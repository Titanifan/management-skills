# Student Triage

Use this module for PhD, DBA, honours, masters, RA, and junior collaborator decisions.

## First principle

Always distinguish supervision duty from strategic investment. The user may owe responsible supervision, clarity, timely feedback, documentation, and fair developmental support without giving a student core identity capital, indefinite rescue labour, or project ownership.

Do not infer project viability from effort alone. Assess direction, rigour, autonomy, learning velocity, response to feedback, and ability to convert work into credible milestones.

## Student classifications

### Platform student

Can help build a long-term arena, dataset, method, theory stream, research platform, or industry/policy connection. Shows autonomy, judgement, learning velocity, and alignment.

Default mode: Own or Build.

### Publication-pathway student

Has a plausible publication pathway without necessarily defining the user's platform. Needs structured milestones and bounded supervision.

Default mode: Build, Option, or Delegate.

### Training student

Primarily requires educational support. Strategic return is limited but teaching or supervision value is legitimate.

Default mode: Service-track.

### Maintenance student

Requires repeated rescue, reframing, monitoring, or emotional and governance labour. May still deserve responsible supervision but not core strategic investment.

Default mode: Service-track, Delegate, Park, or formal intervention.

### Exit or transfer candidate

Topic, capability, motivation, supervision fit, or relationship structure makes continuation harmful or unrealistic. Handle ethically, clearly, and institutionally.

Default mode: redesign, transfer, formal progression intervention, Park, or Kill.

## Student hard kills or escalation triggers

- Persistent non-delivery despite clear milestones.
- No credible thesis or publication pathway.
- Student cannot or will not own the work.
- User is repeatedly forced into rescue, ghostwriting, or project ownership.
- Effort is high but direction, rigour, or response to feedback remains inadequate.
- Topic creates identity dilution or severe portfolio distraction.
- Governance becomes emotionally, ethically, or institutionally risky.
- Continuation delays a better pathway for the student.

## Staged supervision commitment

For uncertain cases specify:

- next 4-8 week milestone;
- evidence standard;
- student-owned deliverable;
- supervisor role and time cap;
- response to success, partial success, or failure;
- documentation and escalation pathway.

Do not repeatedly extend a milestone without changing the decision mode.

## Triage questions

1. What is the student's classification and current stage?
2. What supervision duty remains?
3. What strategic investment is justified?
4. What evidence would upgrade or downgrade the classification?
5. Can the student independently own the next deliverable?
6. What triggers service-track, redesign, transfer, or exit?

## Output emphasis

Be fair and institutionally responsible. Do not recommend abandoning a student casually. Provide boundaries, milestones, documentation, support options, and escalation pathways.
