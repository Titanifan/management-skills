# Learning-loop Calibration

The Adaptive Commitment must improve through use. Treat each judgement as a provisional hypothesis.

## Review schedule

For major decisions, default to:

- **3 months:** early signal and execution validation;
- **6 months:** capability, burden, milestone, and option-value review;
- **12 months:** strategic return, residual assets, and rule calibration.

Use shorter cycles for fragile students, commercial pilots, time-sensitive grants, and high-governance projects.

## Separate four evaluations

Do not collapse them into one outcome judgement.

1. **Decision quality:** Was the original choice reasonable given ex ante evidence?
2. **Execution fidelity:** Were promised resources, boundaries, milestones, and responsibilities actually implemented?
3. **Learning and asset creation:** What decision-relevant information and residual assets were produced?
4. **Realised outcome:** What scholarly, organisational, financial, capability, relationship, or social result occurred?

## Review questions

1. What was the original verdict, mode, stage, and confidence?
2. What assumptions and signals supported it?
3. Was the resource floor met and cap respected?
4. Was the first action taken promptly?
5. Did execution persist without requiring a crisis?
6. Which signals proved valid or misleading?
7. What was underestimated: governance, cognitive capital, data, capability, student autonomy, customer pain, distribution, feasibility, or identity dilution?
8. What evidence, learning, or residual assets were created?
9. Should the opportunity be scaled, continued, redesigned, delegated, parked, killed, or commercialised?
10. Were foregone alternatives realistically assessed, or idealised after the fact?
11. What system rule should change?

## Calibration log

Record:

- date and opportunity;
- original verdict, mode, stage, and confidence;
- key assumptions and signals;
- resource floor and cap;
- milestone and evidence standard;
- review trigger;
- execution fidelity;
- outcome;
- residual assets;
- valid and misleading signals;
- reallocation decision;
- rule update.

## Rule update discipline

Do not rewrite rules because one unusual case succeeded or failed. Update when patterns repeat, or when one failure exposes a severe governance, ethical, or downside risk.

## Counterfactual discipline

Compare realistic full paths using information available at the original decision date. Do not compare the chosen path's actual difficulties with an unchosen path's imagined best outcome.

Use regret only to identify a reusable rule, such as:

- a signal that was overweighted;
- a capability that was missing;
- a commitment that was too small or too large;
- a review that occurred too late;
- an alternative that should have remained an option.

## Common calibration findings

- Interesting ideas are overvalued relative to governance burden.
- Student or collaborator autonomy is overestimated.
- Cognitive capital cost is underestimated.
- Duty is confused with strategic investment.
- Options remain alive without milestones.
- Promising choices receive only leftover time.
- Crisis is used to create commitment.
- Negative evidence is rationalised after identity becomes attached.
- Repeatable methods are under-commercialised.
