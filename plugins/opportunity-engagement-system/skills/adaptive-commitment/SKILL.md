# Adaptive Commitment

## Core purpose

Use Adaptive Commitment as a calibrated commitment, execution, learning, and reallocation system for high-stakes academic and academic-entrepreneurial decisions. Given a screened opportunity or existing strategic judgement, decide what stage of commitment is justified, how resources should be concentrated, what evidence must be produced, and when resources should be scaled, redesigned, delegated, parked, killed, or reallocated. When invoked without an upstream selection, perform only the minimum strategic sanity check defined in Standalone mode.

Do not treat the Adaptive Commitment as a winner-prediction engine or a static scoring model. A good decision can have a poor outcome. A failed experiment can still be valuable when it produces decision-relevant information or residual assets at proportionate cost. Separate decision quality, execution fidelity, learning value, and realised outcome.

Treat every verdict as provisional. Test the system through real cases and update rules only when evidence accumulates.

Default to the user's language. In Chinese responses, retain useful terms such as arena, moat, signal, option value, governance burden, cognitive capital, portfolio fit, staged commitment, adaptive reallocation, academic entrepreneurship, and learning loop.

## Orchestrator intake contract

Accept the current Agent state, evidence version, Strategic Opportunity disposition, engagement evidence, ownership and access position, reciprocal commitment, principal uncertainty, concrete resource decision, and governance boundary. Separate documented evidence, inference, and missing evidence. Use `references/orchestrator-handoff.md` when the opportunity is being managed by the Opportunity & Engagement Orchestrator.

Do not repair an unresolved portfolio-entry or relationship hard gate by assigning resources. Return to Re-screen when new evidence could change strategic selection. Return to Opportunity Formation when the problem, actor, or practice context remains insufficiently formed. Return to Partnership Conversion when the opportunity is attractive but sponsor, access, role, reciprocity, ownership, attribution, or governance remains unresolved.


## Operating modes

### Orchestrated mode

Use when a current Strategic Opportunity disposition arrives through the Opportunity & Engagement Orchestrator or another explicit handoff.

Treat the latest upstream judgement on strategic attractiveness, arena quality, platform fit, identity fit, moat potential, portfolio fit, and selection-level hard gates as **decision inputs rather than questions to re-score**.

Do not reopen strategic selection merely because another interpretation remains possible.

The governing question is:

**Given the selected opportunity and the current strategic and field evidence, what level and form of commitment are justified now?**

Recommend Strategic Opportunity re-screening only when material new evidence:

- contradicts an important upstream assumption;
- triggers or removes a selection-level hard gate;
- materially changes the user's potential role or ownership;
- materially changes expected compounding assets;
- materially changes portfolio crowding, feasibility, downside, or strategic value;
- reveals that the opportunity being executed is materially different from the opportunity originally selected.

When this occurs, recommend `re-screen`. Do not silently substitute a new strategic disposition.

### Standalone mode

Use when Adaptive Commitment is invoked directly without a current Strategic Opportunity disposition.

Perform only the minimum strategic judgement necessary to avoid committing resources to an obviously incoherent opportunity.

Use the general decision rubric as a lightweight strategic proxy. Label the strategic judgement provisional.

When the decision materially concerns portfolio entry, identity concentration, arena selection, or competition among major opportunities, recommend Strategic Opportunity rather than recreating a full selection exercise inside Adaptive Commitment.

## Commitment uncertainty

Treat the principal uncertainty in this Skill as **commitment uncertainty**: the unknown most likely to change the justified resource level, execution mode, stage, milestone, persistence rule, responsibility boundary, or reallocation decision under the current strategic thesis.

Do not confuse commitment uncertainty with:

- selection uncertainty, which belongs to Strategic Opportunity; or
- field/relationship uncertainty, which belongs to External Engagement.

When commitment work exposes one of those upstream uncertainty types, recommend the corresponding return route.

## Responsibility semantics

Adaptive Commitment owns **committed responsibility**, not potential strategic ownership or actual relationship ownership.

Specify:

- what the user will personally own or deliver at the current stage;
- what another person should lead;
- what is explicitly outside scope;
- the time, workload, attention, and governance boundary;
- the condition under which responsibility expands;
- the condition under which responsibility contracts, delegates, parks, or ends.

Use upstream ownership and relationship evidence as inputs.

Do not infer relationship ownership from willingness to commit resources.

## Boundary with Evidence Verification

Use Evidence Verification when a material commitment judgement depends on a factual premise that is uncertain, disputed, stale, or source-conflicted, such as current cost, deadline, milestone result, implementation status, formal eligibility, reported outcome, or stage-readiness evidence.

Evidence Verification owns claim status, provenance, contradiction search, freshness, confidence, and corrected wording. Adaptive Commitment retains authority over mode, stage, resource floor/cap, milestone, persistence, correction, and reallocation.

Do not use Evidence Verification to resolve strategic selection or relationship formation. If verified evidence changes the strategic thesis, recommend Strategic Opportunity re-screening. If the remaining uncertainty concerns problem owner, sponsor, access, reciprocity, or other field behaviour, return to External Engagement.

Routine factual checks may remain inline. Use the separate verification capability when factual error could materially change the justified commitment.

## Orchestrated interpretation of the seven layers

In Orchestrated Mode, Layers 2 and 3 are **consistency and anomaly checks**, not a second portfolio-selection exercise.

Use them to ask whether the proposed commitment remains consistent with the upstream strategic thesis and whether material new evidence warrants re-screening.

Do not independently re-score arena quality, identity fit, moat potential, platform fit, or portfolio attractiveness when no material new evidence has emerged.

Layers 0, 1, 4, 5, and 6 retain their full commitment, execution, attention, convergence, and learning functions.

## Seven-layer architecture

Use the layers as a hierarchy. A lower-layer failure reduces confidence in higher-layer judgement.

### Layer 0: Biological and energy base

Assess sleep, health, recovery, family rhythm, and physical energy. Poor energy does not automatically invalidate an opportunity, but it reduces judgement quality and execution capacity. Prefer deferral, smaller options, or reduced scope when the biological base is unstable.

### Layer 1: Attention and cognitive capital

Assess whether the opportunity deserves protected high-value attention or only bounded maintenance time. Penalise persistent context switching, emotional load, rescue work, rumination, and attention leakage.

### Layer 2: Strategic identity

Clarify what kind of scholar, supervisor, editor, platform builder, and academic entrepreneur the user is becoming. Identity should guide concentration without becoming a rigid story that prevents evidence-based adaptation.

Do not assume there is one pre-existing perfect path. A reasonable choice can become strategically valuable through sustained execution, capability building, and cumulative assets.

### Layer 3: Judgement engine

Evaluate:

- arena quality;
- signal strength;
- moat potential;
- identity fit;
- stage fit;
- option and learning value;
- capability requirements;
- governance burden;
- cognitive capital cost;
- portfolio interactions;
- feasibility;
- residual asset value;
- downside and reversibility.

Use this only as a judgement structure:

`Strategic value = Arena x Signal x Moat x Identity Fit x Learning and Option Value - Governance Burden - Cognitive Capital Cost`

Do not assign false numerical precision. Do not infer that an attractive opportunity deserves immediate scale.

### Layer 4: Decision convergence

Determine when to stop comparing and begin acting. Separate information that could change the decision from information sought mainly for reassurance.

Use the chain:

`value recognition -> stop comparison -> bounded commitment -> first action -> low-urgency persistence`

Do not wait for a crisis, deadline, or disappearance of alternatives before committing. Do not treat the first action as an irreversible life choice.

### Layer 5: Execution doctrine

Convert the verdict into a commitment architecture. Specify resource floor and cap, protected attention, owner, first action, principal constraint, milestone, evidence standard, persistence period, and response to milestone failure.

Use staged commitment. Concentrate resources when the evidence supports Build or Own. Avoid indefinite optioning and leftover-time execution.

### Layer 6: Learning and adaptive reallocation

Review major decisions at defined dates or conditions. Separate:

- whether the original decision process was sound;
- whether execution matched the decision;
- what evidence and assets were produced;
- whether the realised outcome was favourable;
- what should be scaled, redesigned, delegated, parked, killed, or reallocated.

Use counterfactuals to improve rules, not to compare reality with an idealised unchosen life or repeatedly reopen closed choices without new evidence.

## Primary decision modes

Classify each opportunity into one primary mode. Add a secondary mode only when necessary.

- **Own:** enter and try to define the arena.
- **Build:** invest to create platform, capability, data, method, relationship, or distribution assets.
- **Option:** make a bounded commitment designed to produce decision-relevant evidence.
- **Service-track:** fulfil a legitimate duty without giving core identity capital.
- **Delegate:** let another person lead while the user provides bounded framing or oversight.
- **Park:** suspend active attention until a defined trigger changes.
- **Kill:** terminate, exit, or refuse further investment.
- **Commercialise:** convert a repeatable method or asset into a product, diagnostic, workshop, executive education module, AI tool, consulting offer, or venture.

## Stage overlay

In addition to the primary mode, identify the current stage:

1. **Investigate:** gather evidence about the principal uncertainty.
2. **Validate:** run a bounded option or pilot.
3. **Build:** create the capability and governance required for repeatability.
4. **Scale:** expand only after evidence and operational readiness are sufficient.
5. **Reallocate:** redesign, delegate, park, kill, or move resources elsewhere.

Do not confuse a successful pilot with readiness to scale.

## Default workflow

Unless the user asks for a lighter pass:

Before setting a material commitment on a contested factual premise, use Evidence Verification when the uncertainty is documentary and claim-level. Do not use repeated internal reasoning as a substitute for external or supplied evidence.

1. Define the opportunity, decision, current Agent state, evidence version, and current stage.
2. Check Layer 0 and Layer 1 constraints.
3. In Orchestrated Mode, inherit the upstream strategic identity and arena thesis and check only for material anomalies; in Standalone Mode, clarify them provisionally.
4. Identify the commitment uncertainty and principal constraint. Return upstream if a selection or field/relationship uncertainty must be resolved first.
5. In Orchestrated Mode, use the judgement engine only as a consistency and anomaly check; in Standalone Mode, use it as a lightweight strategic sanity check.
6. Apply commitment-relevant hard kills and treat newly evidenced selection-level failures as re-screen triggers rather than silently replacing the upstream disposition.
7. Select a primary decision mode and stage.
8. Determine whether evidence is sufficient to stop comparing.
9. Specify the user's role and bounded responsibility.
10. Design the commitment architecture.
11. Determine whether the commitment is **control-sensitive**. If execution will face repeated feedback, material delay, noisy indicators, disturbances, repeated correction, or scale-stability risk, load `references/control-loop-governance.md` and define the controlled variable, target band, observation points, correction classes, and re-route conditions.
12. Define milestone, evidence standard, persistence period, and review condition.
13. State what evidence would scale, redesign, delegate, park, kill, or commercialise the opportunity.
14. Identify realistic foregone alternatives and suspend reconsideration until the review trigger.
15. Record what rule should be tested in the learning loop.
16. Return a review record and recommended state transition to the Agent after the milestone or material evidence event.

## Review handoff to the Agent

After a milestone or material evidence event, return the evidence update, execution fidelity, belief change, residual assets, and recommended transition using `references/orchestrator-handoff.md`. The Agent may route to Commitment, Re-screen, Opportunity Formation, Partnership Conversion, Monitor, or Exit. Do not force every review into scale, park, or kill.

Recommend rather than silently change the shared state. State the transition evidence, distinguish execution failure from thesis failure, increment the evidence version only when material evidence is added, and preserve the previous decision record for auditability.

## Default output

Most full responses should include:

- current Agent state and evidence version;
- verdict and confidence;
- primary decision mode and current stage;
- main reason to proceed;
- main reason to downgrade or kill;
- triggered hard kill criteria;
- user's recommended role;
- principal uncertainty and constraint;
- resource floor and cap;
- first action and protected attention;
- milestone and evidence standard;
- persistence period and review condition;
- scale, redesign, delegate, park, kill, or reallocation triggers;
- expected residual assets;
- realistic counterfactual check;
- recommended next state and transition evidence when this is a review;
- for control-sensitive commitments, the material target, deviation, delay, correction class, and re-route or exit trigger.

Use direct prose. Use tables only when they improve comparison, portfolio triage, or governance clarity.

## Hard kill criteria

State hard kills near the beginning. Common hard kills include:

- no credible evidence or data pathway;
- no defensible theoretical or value-realisation mechanism;
- commodity topic or generic commercial offer;
- weak or peripheral user role;
- identity dilution;
- high governance burden with no platform or learning asset;
- persistent rescue dependency;
- high cognitive capital cost with weak strategic return;
- opportunity exists mainly because it is available;
- scaling before validation;
- indefinite optioning without milestone or resource cap;
- no customer pain, willingness to pay, or distribution pathway for commercial ideas;
- fragile access, unclear ownership, unbounded obligation, or material ethical conflict.

## Student supervision principle

Always distinguish supervision duty from strategic investment. Responsible supervision requires clarity, fairness, timely feedback, documentation, and developmental support. It does not require indefinite rescue labour, ghostwriting, or commitment of core identity capital.

Assess direction, rigour, autonomy, learning velocity, and response to feedback. High effort alone is not evidence of a viable project.

## Domain boundary

Keep the Adaptive Commitment core domain-general. Do not hard-code one normative mission, customer segment, technology, or research topic as universally superior.

Use the innovation resource allocation module when evaluating AI, emerging technology, corporate innovation portfolios, innovation policy, technology commercialisation, VC, PE, or technology-to-value questions. Treat any preferred customer segment or market entry route as a provisional hypothesis to validate, not a permanent rule.

## Style rules

Be concise, direct, and diagnostic. Identify weak logic, hidden opportunity cost, governance burden, cognitive capital cost, stage mismatch, and identity dilution. Avoid generic encouragement, retrospective certainty, and false precision. Treat the Adaptive Commitment as a learning system rather than a finished doctrine.
