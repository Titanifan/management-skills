# Strategic Choice Memo Template

Use this structure for a full opportunity-selection evaluation. Adapt length to the user's request. Add the Zero-to-One section only for management-practice, commercialisation, product, platform, investment, or venture opportunities.

## 1. Verdict

State one portfolio disposition:

- core candidate;
- selective candidate;
- service-only;
- monitor;
- redesign;
- reject.

Include confidence: high, medium, or low.

## 2. Opportunity definition

Define the opportunity in one or two sentences. Separate the surface topic from the underlying arena and selection question.

## 3. Strategic diagnosis

Cover:

- non-consensus insight or distinctive value mechanism;
- arena potential;
- theoretical or practical mechanism;
- evidence pathway;
- identity and moat implications;
- user's role, control, and ownership;
- principal uncertainty and binding weakness.

## 4. Kill criteria

List triggered general, practice-impact, or venture hard kills. Explain whether redesign can remove them.

## 6. Platform and portfolio decision

Classify as:

- platform-core;
- platform-adjacent;
- legacy/opportunistic.

Then assign one portfolio disposition:

- core candidate;
- selective candidate;
- service-only;
- monitor;
- redesign;
- reject.

Explain interactions with existing projects, cognitive capital, governance burden, and foregone alternatives.

### 7.6 Caps, overrides, and dual-track class

List:

- score caps;
- market-truth problems;
- power-law limits;
- definite-plan weaknesses;
- foundation and ownership defects;
- classification: Build-to-Own Candidate, Impact Practice, Venture Hypothesis, or Redesign/Reject.

## 8. Counterfactual check

Compare the recommended portfolio disposition with the strongest realistic alternative. Include benefits, costs, risk, transition burden, lost options, governance, ownership, and strategic crowding for both. Do not compare reality with an idealised alternative.

## 9. Redesign recommendation

When promising but weak, provide:

- revised question, decision, or value proposition;
- stronger mechanism;
- narrower user, buyer, or beachhead;
- required evidence or capability;
- user's appropriate role and ownership position;
- evidence that would materially raise or lower the strategic verdict.

Do not prescribe execution steps here.

## 10. Adaptive Commitment handoff

Do not create the commitment architecture. Transfer:

- selected opportunity definition;
- platform classification;
- portfolio disposition;
- Zero-to-One class, when applicable;
- current evidence stage;
- principal uncertainty;
- binding constraint;
- evidence that would strengthen or weaken the opportunity;
- expected residual assets;
- major governance or ownership constraints.

Adaptive Commitment should determine execution mode and stage, resources, protected time, first action, milestones, persistence, review, and reallocation.
