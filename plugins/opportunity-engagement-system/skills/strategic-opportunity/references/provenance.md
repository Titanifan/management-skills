# Provenance Map for Strategic Opportunity

## Purpose and claim boundary

Use this file to distinguish the origins of Strategic Opportunity from the design of its executable workflow. Sources provide constructs, mechanisms, or practitioner lenses; they do not validate the complete Skill, its scores, its verdicts, or its application to an individual academic career.

Most scholarly sources theorise firms, organisational units, or corporate managers. Applying them to a scholar's opportunity portfolio is a cross-level translation. Treat scores as disciplined elicitation devices, not estimated causal models.

## Provenance labels

- `Scholarly-direct`: the source directly develops the named construct, mechanism, or distinction.
- `Scholarly-translated`: the executable rule adapts scholarship across level or context.
- `Practitioner-derived`: the rule comes from a practitioner framework whose assumptions must be tested.
- `Authorial-synthesis`: the repository combines sources into a workflow, score, threshold, classification, or artefact.
- `Case-calibrated`: repeated documented cases support a behaviour under stated conditions. No v0.2 element has this status.

## Source-to-rule map

| Element | Provenance | What the source supports | Executable use in this Skill | Boundary condition or failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strategy as an evolving pattern | `Scholarly-direct` | Strategies can be understood as patterns in streams of decisions, with deliberate and emergent forms (Mintzberg, 1978; Mintzberg & Waters, 1985). | Examine the path of commitments and assets, not only the stated plan. | Retrospective pattern recognition does not determine the best opportunity ex ante. |
| Exploration and exploitation | `Scholarly-direct` | Organisational learning involves competing exploration and exploitation demands (March, 1991). | Preserve exploration without granting every uncertain idea core status. | The source does not prescribe a universal portfolio balance or an individual allocation rule. |
| Resource heterogeneity and defensibility | `Scholarly-direct` | Valuable, rare, difficult-to-imitate, and non-substitutable resources can underpin sustained advantage (Barney, 1991). | Ask which reusable assets an opportunity could create. | A personal reputation, relationship, or dataset is not automatically a defensible firm resource. |
| Attention structures | `Scholarly-direct` | Organisational rules and relationships shape which issues and answers receive attention (Ocasio, 1997). | Treat attention crowding and repeated issue exposure as selection costs. | Attention is organisationally structured and cannot always be reallocated by individual choice. |
| Behavioural bounds and cognitively distant opportunities | `Scholarly-direct` | Behavioural strategy examines how cognition shapes search for strategically valuable opportunities (Gavetti, 2012). | Test whether an overlooked opportunity is both non-consensus and accessible to this actor. | Novelty or cognitive distance is not evidence of value. |
| Dynamic managerial capabilities | `Scholarly-direct` | Managerial cognition, human capital, and social capital can contribute to heterogeneous strategic outcomes (Adner & Helfat, 2003). | Examine actor-specific capability and access rather than abstract opportunity quality alone. | Current capability can be built, borrowed, or delegated; corporate-level evidence does not validate a personal-fit score. |
| Internal venturing and strategic context | `Scholarly-direct` | Internal initiatives need selection and strategic context to influence corporate strategy (Burgelman, 1983). | Distinguish a locally interesting initiative from one that can secure ownership, legitimacy, and resources. | Internal corporate venturing does not transfer automatically to independent ventures, policy, or academic work. |
| Sequential investment under uncertainty | `Scholarly-direct` | Real-options reasoning can inform incremental positioning investments under uncertainty (McGrath, 1997). | Prefer opportunities whose central uncertainty can be tested before irreversible build. | Calling an activity an option does not make it cheap, reversible, informative, or worth preserving. |
| Opportunity-seeking plus advantage-seeking | `Scholarly-direct` | Strategic entrepreneurship integrates entrepreneurial opportunity-seeking with strategic advantage-seeking (Hitt et al., 2001; Ireland et al., 2003). | Require both a credible opportunity and a path to durable value creation or capture. | These models do not validate the Skill's scores or Peter Thiel's seven questions. |
| Opportunity discovery versus creation | `Scholarly-direct` | Discovery and creation theories imply different assumptions and entrepreneurial actions (Alvarez & Barney, 2007). | Ask whether evidence can precede action or whether bounded action must help create the opportunity. | A case can contain both logics; the distinction does not itself determine whether the opportunity deserves entry. |
| Opportunity-belief updating | `Scholarly-direct` | Opportunity beliefs develop as actors reduce ignorance and doubt through signals, knowledge, and further evaluation (Shepherd et al., 2007). | Treat a strategic judgement as an evidence-indexed belief that can be revised when field evidence changes. | The source explains belief formation; it does not validate this Skill's states, evidence versions, thresholds, or routing rules. |
| Stocks, flows, and feedback structure | `Scholarly-direct` | Systems dynamics distinguishes accumulated state from rates of change and explains behaviour through interacting reinforcing and balancing feedback (Meadows, 2008; Sterman, 2000). | When system structure matters, identify the key stocks, flows, dominant loops, and the accumulated assets or constraints that govern the opportunity. | A qualitative map is a model hypothesis, not causal proof; omitted variables and boundary choices can change the story. |
| Delays, dynamic complexity, and moving constraints | `Scholarly-direct` | Delays and endogenous feedback can generate overshoot, oscillation, misperception, and counterintuitive system behaviour (Meadows, 2008; Sterman, 2000). | Test whether observed signals are lagged, whether scaling moves the bottleneck, and whether a favourable intervention creates a new balancing constraint. | The presence of delay does not specify its magnitude or guarantee a particular dynamic pattern. |
| Leverage points | `Scholarly-direct` | Meadows (1999, 2008) distinguishes intervention points ranging from parameters and flows to information, feedback, rules, goals, and governing assumptions. | Rank where an opportunity intervenes and test whether it changes a symptom, a constraint, a feedback structure, a rule, or the system's objective. | Deeper leverage is not automatically preferable; authority, ethics, feasibility, and unintended consequences may make a narrower intervention superior. |
| Systems-sensitive opportunity diagnosis | `Authorial-synthesis` | Systems-dynamics concepts motivate a structured diagnosis of boundaries, stocks, loops, delays, constraints, endogenous response, and leverage. | Load the systems-thinking reference only when system structure could materially change portfolio selection; carry forward only findings that affect the strategic verdict. | The trigger rule, output template, opportunity translations, and Skill routing are repository design choices and require case calibration. |
| Personal arena and compounding-asset rule | `Scholarly-translated` | Strategy-pattern, resource, attention, and capability mechanisms jointly motivate cumulative path analysis. | Prefer opportunities that deepen an arena and create reusable assets rather than isolated activity. | This synthesis crosses from firm and organisation levels to a personal portfolio and requires case calibration. |
| Actor-specific opportunity fit | `Scholarly-translated` | Dynamic managerial capabilities and attention research make actor heterogeneity and constraints relevant. | Assess the user's role, access, ownership, capability, and cognitive cost. | A weak current fit can sometimes be redesigned; a strong fit does not make a weak opportunity attractive. |
| Option-preserving selection rule | `Scholarly-translated` | Real-options logic supports sequential commitments when learning is possible. | Route a positive or provisional opportunity to the smallest next capability that can resolve its governing uncertainty rather than designing execution here. | Additional stages can create delay, ritual, and governance cost without useful learning. |
| Seven venture questions | `Practitioner-derived` | Thiel and Masters (2014) offer an adversarial venture lens covering engineering, timing, monopoly, people, distribution, durability, and secret. | Stress-test venture architecture after the opportunity passes the rapid screen. | This is not peer-reviewed validation. The lens favours concentration, private value capture, founder control, and monopoly-oriented reasoning. |
| Strategic score, thresholds, and dispositions | `Authorial-synthesis` | No listed source specifies this scoring system. | Structure comparison and force an explicit portfolio disposition. | Numerical precision can conceal weak evidence; hard gates and narrative diagnosis override totals. |
| PIP/ZVP dual-track architecture | `Authorial-synthesis` | The repository combines practice-impact tests with the practitioner venture lens. | Keep public or organisational impact separate from scalable, defensible, ownable venture potential. | Neither track has been validated as a unified predictive model. |
| Score weights, caps, overrides, and two-by-two classes | `Authorial-synthesis` | These are repository design choices. | Prevent absent buyers, weak evidence, false market definitions, or unavailable ownership from being averaged away. | Thresholds require forward tests and case calibration; they must not be presented as empirical cut-offs. |
| Preliminary screen and re-screen routing boundary | `Authorial-synthesis` | Opportunity-belief updating, discovery/creation, and sequential-investment research jointly motivate revisable selection under uncertainty. | Run a preliminary screen before field work, then re-screen the prior judgement against a new evidence version after material relationship or problem evidence changes; route according to the governing uncertainty. | No cited source directly validates the preliminary-screen/re-screen labels, the evidence-version protocol, or the handoff boundary among Strategic Opportunity, External Engagement, and Adaptive Commitment. |

## Calibration rules

- Do not convert a citation into authority for a specific verdict.
- Do not infer causality from conceptual or process research.
- Do not treat a firm-level construct as automatically valid at the individual level.
- Do not let adjacent scholarship academically legitimise a practitioner thesis.
- Record decision quality separately from realised outcome.
- Reserve `Case-calibrated` for a documented series of comparable cases, not one vivid success.

## Strategic heuristics intelligence layer

| Element | Provenance | What the source or tradition supports | Executable use in this Skill | Boundary condition or failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First-principles decomposition | `Authorial-synthesis` informed by analytical reasoning traditions | Complex claims can be decomposed into assumptions, necessary conditions, constraints, and mechanisms before evaluation. | Break an opportunity into the minimum conditions that must hold and separate fact, assumption, field hypothesis, and resource-backed test. | "First principles" is not a licence to ignore accumulated empirical evidence, institutions, history, or domain expertise. |
| Investigation before judgement | `Practitioner-derived` / `Scholarly-translated` | Practice-oriented strategic traditions emphasise understanding concrete conditions before applying general prescriptions. | Require a concrete situation diagnosis before using fashionable labels or generic playbooks. | Historical political or military contexts do not validate universal business or academic rules. |
| Primary contradiction translated as governing strategic tension | `Practitioner-derived` / `Authorial-synthesis` | Selected contradiction-analysis traditions distinguish more central from less central tensions in a changing situation. | Identify the tension or constraint most likely to govern the current strategic verdict and distinguish it from next-order constraints. | Do not import ideological categories or assume all organisational problems are antagonistic contradictions. |
| Concentration of effort | `Practitioner-derived` / `Scholarly-translated` | Strategy traditions commonly emphasise concentrating scarce resources at consequential points rather than spreading them uniformly. | Identify the one to three evidence points, relationships, capabilities, or assets most likely to change the selection verdict. | Strategic Opportunity identifies concentration points but does not set resource amounts; over-concentration can create fragility or illegitimate zero-sum behaviour. |
| Local advantage and sequencing | `Practitioner-derived` / `Authorial-synthesis` | Narrower positions and ordered moves can reduce uncertainty and build advantage before broader expansion. | Test whether a defensible beachhead or sequence can create evidence and compounding assets before scale. | Artificially narrow markets or indefinite sequencing can hide weak demand or delay necessary commitment. |
| Practice-feedback-revision | `Practitioner-derived` / `Scholarly-translated` | Practice-oriented epistemologies treat action and feedback as sources of learning when observation is insufficient. | Route bounded field interaction to External Engagement and material resource-backed tests to Adaptive Commitment when action is needed to update a belief. | Activity is not learning unless a specific belief can change; sunk effort and emotional attachment are not evidence. |
| Non-zero-sum and legitimacy correction | `Authorial-synthesis` | The repository deliberately limits transfer of conflict-derived heuristics into cooperative, public-interest, academic, and relational settings. | Test whether competition is genuinely rivalrous before using adversarial framing; preserve ethics, legitimacy, reciprocity, and institutional constraints. | This is a design safeguard, not a claim derived from a single historical source. |

### Strategic-heuristics source boundary

The `strategic-heuristics.md` reference is not a summary of *Selected Works of Mao Zedong*, nor does it treat Maoist political or military strategy as a universal management theory. It extracts a narrow set of reusable prompts—concrete investigation, identifying the governing tension, distinguishing primary from secondary constraints, concentrating effort, sequencing, and learning through action—then translates them into non-coercive strategic decision rules. Historical, ideological, revolutionary, and adversarial assumptions are not carried forward unless independently justified by the contemporary context.

## Technology possibility-set intelligence layer

| Element | Provenance | What the source or tradition supports | Executable use in this Skill | Boundary condition or failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Technology can change patterns of action, scale, pace, and coordination | `Practitioner-derived` / `Scholarly-translated` | Media-effects reasoning associated with McLuhan directs attention to how a medium changes social and organisational patterns rather than only the content carried through it. | Ask whether technology changes the feasible action set rather than merely improving the old task. | McLuhan does not provide the Skill's possibility-set taxonomy, market validation rules, or value-capture tests. |
| Affordance-sensitive analysis | `Scholarly-translated` | Technology-affordance research distinguishes properties of technology from action possibilities that emerge in relation to actors, goals, and context. | Identify which actions become newly feasible for which actors, under which complements and constraints. | Technical capability alone does not establish use, adoption, demand, legitimacy, or impact. |
| Complementarity and organisational change | `Scholarly-translated` | Information-technology and organisation research shows that value often depends on complementary organisational change rather than technology alone. | Identify required workflow, skill, governance, data, infrastructure, and decision-right complements. | Complementarity can create long delays and implementation burden that erase nominal technical value. |
| Moving bottleneck after technical progress | `Authorial-synthesis` informed by systems thinking | Removing one constraint can expose another binding constraint. | Ask what becomes scarce after the technology makes the old task cheap, fast, visible, scalable, or automatable. | The next bottleneck is a hypothesis until supported by field or operational evidence. |
| Capability window | `Authorial-synthesis` | Technology can advance faster than complementary capabilities, institutions, standards, procurement, or skills. | Test whether temporary mismatch creates an opportunity to build a distinctive capability or interface position. | A window can close quickly; its existence and duration require evidence. |
| Interface control and value capture | `Scholarly-translated` / `Authorial-synthesis` | Strategy research broadly distinguishes value creation from value capture and highlights control over complements, interfaces, and distribution. | Identify who controls the user relationship, workflow, data, standards, distribution, and integration burden. | Technical contribution can remain strategically weak when another actor owns the interface or captures the value. |

### Technology-possibility source boundary

`technology-possibility-set.md` is not a McLuhan interpretation guide. McLuhan is used only as an intellectual prompt to ask how a new medium or technology changes patterns of action, scale, pace, and coordination. The taxonomy of automation, augmentation, coordination, information visibility, temporal compression, scale, personalisation, modularity, agency, and transaction possibilities is an authorial synthesis informed by broader technology and organisation research. Treat all possibility claims as hypotheses until supported by evidence.
