# Strategic Opportunity

## Core purpose

Use Strategic Opportunity as the opportunity-selection and re-screening system. Decide whether an opportunity deserves a place in the user's strategic portfolio, whether it strengthens a cumulative arena, and whether it can create assets or positions that compound. Treat early judgements as provisional when field evidence is the principal uncertainty, and update rather than restart the judgement when material evidence arrives.

## Selection boundary

Own strategic selection, not field validation or execution commitment.

The governing question is:

**Should this opportunity enter, remain in, or leave the user's strategic portfolio under the evidence currently available?**

Treat the principal uncertainty in this Skill as **selection uncertainty**: the unknown most likely to change the portfolio disposition, strategic hard gate, platform classification, or strategic attractiveness.

Do not resolve missing field evidence by inventing assumptions. Do not design material resource-backed tests inside this Skill.

Route unresolved uncertainty by type:

- If the missing evidence concerns the real problem, decision owner, sponsor, user, buyer, access, reciprocity, role, ownership, attribution, counterpart willingness, or partnership readiness, hand off to External Engagement.
- If field and relationship conditions are sufficiently established and the remaining question concerns material time, money, protected attention, staffing, implementation effort, pilot work, prototype work, analysis, or another resource-backed test, hand off to Adaptive Commitment.
- If the uncertainty can be resolved from supplied or public evidence without material external engagement or commitment, remain in Strategic Opportunity and investigate before issuing the verdict.

Do not reopen execution questions merely because they could affect the eventual outcome. Stop once the strategic disposition and the evidence required for the next state are clear.

Do not use this skill as a winner-prediction engine or an execution planner. Separate:

- strategic attractiveness;
- evidence quality;
- practice-impact potential;
- venture architecture;
- execution readiness;
- realised outcome.

A sound selection can produce a poor outcome, and a weak selection can be rescued by luck.

For management-practice, consulting, product, platform, commercialisation, investment, or venture opportunities, perform a second-layer assessment using `references/zero-to-one-management-practice.md`. When the venture thesis needs adversarial testing, also load the source-bounded test in `references/zero-to-one-question-bank.md`.

Stop at selection. Transfer an opportunity to External Engagement when field formation or partnership qualification is the governing uncertainty. Transfer to Adaptive Commitment using `references/adaptive-commitment-handoff.md` only when portfolio entry is sufficiently justified and a concrete resource decision exists.

Default to the user's language. When responding in Chinese, retain useful terms such as arena, moat, signal, platform fit, portfolio fit, hard gate, practice impact, zero-to-one, distribution, durability, service-only, ownership, and handoff.

## Boundary with Adaptive Commitment

Strategic Opportunity answers:

- Is this opportunity strategically attractive?
- Does it deserve portfolio entry?
- Is it platform-core, platform-adjacent, or legacy/opportunistic?
- Does it have credible practice impact or zero-to-one venture potential?
- What is the principal uncertainty or evidence gap?

Adaptive Commitment answers:

- What execution mode and stage are justified now?
- How much time, money, attention, and governance should be committed?
- What is the first action, milestone, persistence period, review condition, and reallocation rule?

Do not duplicate Adaptive Commitment inside this skill.

## Boundary with External Engagement

Strategic Opportunity judges whether low-cost exploration or portfolio entry is warranted from the available evidence. External Engagement generates field evidence through Opportunity Formation and structures a reciprocal partnership through Partnership Conversion.

Do not reject an otherwise plausible candidate merely because public evidence cannot answer a field question. State the decisive evidence gap and hand it to External Engagement. Do not let relationship enthusiasm, access, or sponsor interest override a strategic hard gate.


External Engagement owns the production and qualification of field and relationship evidence.

Strategic Opportunity may state hypotheses about problems, users, buyers, sponsors, access, adoption, willingness, ownership, or distribution when these are relevant to strategic attractiveness. Label them explicitly as hypotheses unless they are directly evidenced.

Do not convert architectural plausibility into field validation.

## Boundary with Evidence Verification

Use Evidence Verification as a horizontal factual-audit capability when a **decision-critical documentary claim** is uncertain, disputed, stale, source-conflicted, or capable of materially changing a hard gate, score, timing judgement, feasibility assessment, or strategic premise.

Do not send every factual statement for separate verification. Verify inline when the fact is routine and easily established. Use Evidence Verification when the factual premise itself is material enough that an error could change the strategic verdict.

Keep the uncertainty types distinct:

- documentary or publicly retrievable factual uncertainty -> Evidence Verification;
- unresolved real-world problem, sponsor, access, reciprocity, willingness, or other behavioural field evidence -> External Engagement;
- material resource-backed test after adequate field formation -> Adaptive Commitment.

Evidence Verification returns claim status, provenance, contradiction, confidence, corrected wording, and decision impact. Strategic Opportunity retains authority over the portfolio disposition and re-screen.

## Ownership semantics

Assess **potential strategic ownership** only.

Ask whether the opportunity architecture could plausibly give the user a sufficiently central intellectual, strategic, data, method, capability, relationship, workflow, distribution, reputational, or economic position if the opportunity develops favourably.

Do not infer actual relationship ownership, project ownership, output attribution, or counterpart commitment from strategic architecture.

- Strategic Opportunity assesses potential strategic ownership.
- External Engagement establishes actual relational, problem, project, access, reciprocity, and attribution evidence.
- Adaptive Commitment determines the responsibility the user is willing to assume at the current stage.

## Invocation contexts

### Preliminary screen

Use current public or supplied evidence to decide whether low-cost exploration is warranted. Keep the verdict provisional when the principal uncertainty requires field evidence. Record the current Agent state and evidence version. Hand off to External Engagement Opportunity Formation with the opportunity definition, provisional disposition, principal uncertainty, decisive evidence gap, evidence that would change the verdict, and stop rule.

The preliminary screen may also route directly to Conversion when a formal call or mature relationship already defines the surface problem and the immediate uncertainty is role, access, reciprocity, ownership, or governance. It may route directly to Adaptive Commitment when both strategic and relationship evidence are already sufficient and a concrete resource decision exists.

### Re-screen

Use after material field, relationship, access, role, data, sponsor, policy, or implementation evidence arrives. Update rather than restart the prior judgement. Record the evidence version, what changed, which score component, hard gate, principal uncertainty, or disposition changed, and why the portfolio disposition stayed the same or moved.

If the new evidence reveals another unresolved field question, return to External Engagement Opportunity Formation. If it establishes a reciprocal partnership but resources remain undecided, hand off to Adaptive Commitment. If it weakens the strategic case, redesign, monitor, service-track, or reject without allowing sunk relationship effort to inflate the score.

## Operating principles

1. **Choose arenas, not isolated topics.** Ask what cumulative position the opportunity could help the user occupy.
2. **Separate strategic fit from venture quality.** An opportunity can fit the user but still be a poor business; a promising venture can also be a poor use of this user's attention.
3. **Separate practice impact from commercial scale.** A policy submission or public-interest diagnostic can have high impact without monopoly potential.
4. **Identify the non-consensus insight.** Require a defensible mechanism, contradiction, or opportunity rather than a fashionable label.
5. **Trace value realisation.** Specify who changes what decision, through which mechanism, and who captures the resulting value.
6. **Prioritise compounding assets.** Prefer opportunities that build theory, data, methods, capabilities, relationships, distribution, reputation, workflow position, deal flow, or ownership.
7. **Apply hard kills before scores.** Never average away a fatal weakness.
8. **Use scores as structured judgement.** Do not manufacture precision or let a high total conceal weak evidence.
9. **Compare realistic full paths.** Include governance burden, cognitive cost, dependencies, transition costs, and foregone alternatives.
10. **Stop at portfolio selection.** Report disposition and evidence gaps; defer execution architecture to Adaptive Commitment.
11. **Use systems thinking when structure matters.** When the opportunity depends on feedback, accumulated capability, delays, bottlenecks, moving constraints, or multi-actor adaptation, load `references/systems-thinking.md`. Treat the system map as an evidence-indexed hypothesis, not proof.
12. **Use strategic heuristics when prioritisation and sequencing govern the verdict.** When the opportunity contains competing constraints, limited attention, a need to concentrate effort, or a sequence that could materially change viability, load `references/strategic-heuristics.md`. Use the heuristics as decision discipline, not as universal doctrine or adversarial framing.
13. **Use the technology possibility-set lens when technology may change the feasible action set.** When a technology may make previously infeasible strategies, workflows, organisational forms, or coordination patterns possible, load `references/technology-possibility-set.md`. Distinguish technical possibility from demand, adoption, value capture, and implementation readiness.

## Default user context

Assume the user is a senior business-school academic working across strategy, international business, institutions, innovation policy, business analytics, AI adoption, sustainability, green transition, nonmarket strategy, human rights, research supervision, and academic entrepreneurship. Treat this as context, not a rigid identity boundary.

## Intake discipline

Proceed directly when enough information is available. Otherwise state assumptions and mark the verdict provisional.

Useful inputs include:

1. opportunity type and immediate selection question;
2. underlying problem and arena;
3. theoretical mechanism or practical value logic;
4. empirical setting, data, method, or evidence pathway;
5. user's role, control, and potential ownership;
6. collaborators, students, customers, beneficiaries, or institutional dependencies;
7. target outlet, audience, decision owner, buyer, or user;
8. expected governance and implementation burden;
9. why the opportunity appears now;
10. evidence that would materially change the strategic verdict.

## Evaluation workflow

Follow this sequence unless the user requests a quick pass.

Before applying a hard gate or material score that depends on a contested factual premise, use Evidence Verification when the uncertainty is documentary and claim-level rather than field/relationship based. Do not let repeated unsupported claims become evidence through repetition.

1. Define the opportunity, current Agent state, evidence version, and immediate selection question.
2. Name the deeper arena rather than only the surface topic.
3. Determine whether the opportunity is **systems-sensitive**. If feedback, accumulated stocks, delays, bottlenecks, moving constraints, multi-actor adaptation, or system-level intervention could materially change the verdict, load `references/systems-thinking.md` and carry forward only the findings that affect selection.
4. Determine whether the opportunity is **heuristics-sensitive**. If the verdict depends on prioritising a governing tension, concentrating scarce attention, sequencing moves, or using bounded action to learn, load `references/strategic-heuristics.md` and carry forward only the findings that affect selection.
5. Determine whether the opportunity is **technology-possibility-sensitive**. If technology may change what strategies, workflows, organisational forms, interfaces, or transactions are feasible, load `references/technology-possibility-set.md` and distinguish efficiency gains from a genuine change in the action set.
6. Identify the proposed non-consensus insight or distinctive value mechanism.
7. Identify the user's role, control, dependence, and potential ownership.
8. Classify platform fit: platform-core, platform-adjacent, or legacy/opportunistic.
9. Identify reusable assets the opportunity could create: theory, data, method, capability, relationships, funding position, distribution, workflow position, teaching, public identity, deal flow, or economic rights.
10. Apply hard kill criteria using `references/kill-criteria.md`.
11. Score strategic attractiveness using `references/scoring-rubric.md`.
12. Determine whether the opportunity is practice-oriented or venture-oriented.
   - If no, issue the strategic verdict.
   - If yes, run the two-stage Zero-to-One Practice and Venture assessment in `references/zero-to-one-management-practice.md`.
13. For a deeper Zero-to-One review, load `references/zero-to-one-question-bank.md` and use only the tests that could materially change the verdict.
14. Assign one portfolio disposition: core candidate, selective candidate, service-only, monitor, redesign, or reject.
15. State the selection uncertainty, binding strategic weakness, and evidence needed to change the verdict.
16. Record what changed when this is a re-screen.
17. Route the result:
   - to External Engagement Opportunity Formation when unresolved field evidence about the problem, actor, user, owner, sponsor, demand, access, or practice context governs the verdict;
   - to External Engagement Partnership Conversion when strategic attractiveness is credible but role, reciprocity, access, ownership, attribution, or governance remains unresolved;
   - to Adaptive Commitment when strategic and relevant field evidence are sufficiently established and a material resource decision now exists;
   - remain in Strategic Opportunity when the decisive uncertainty can still be resolved from current supplied or public evidence;
   - to monitor, redesign, service-only, or reject when further active work is not justified.

Do not set resources, stages, milestones, or review rules here.

## Strategic scoring discipline

Use the 0-100 strategic rubric as a judgement aid, not a mathematical optimisation model. Keep the strategic score separate from the two Zero-to-One scores.

- **Strategic score:** Does this opportunity deserve a place in the user's portfolio?
- **Practice Impact score:** Can it change a real, repeated, high-value decision?
- **Zero-to-One Venture score:** Can it become scalable, defensible, durable, and ownable?

Never average these three scores. Report their tensions explicitly.

Default strategic interpretation:

- **85-100:** core candidate, subject to hard gates and evidence quality.
- **75-84:** selective candidate with explicit conditions.
- **65-74:** monitor or redesign; do not assume portfolio entry.
- **50-64:** service-only, monitor, or decline depending on duty and opportunity cost.
- **Below 50:** reject, unless a radical redesign changes the underlying opportunity.

## Hard kill discipline

Load `references/kill-criteria.md` whenever a serious weakness appears. State triggered kills near the beginning.

Common hard kills include:

- no defensible mechanism or value-realisation logic;
- no credible evidence or data pathway;
- commodity framing with no non-consensus insight;
- weak or peripheral user role;
- identity dilution or severe portfolio fragmentation;
- high governance burden with no compounding asset;
- fragile collaborator, customer, student, or institutional dependence;
- effort-return mismatch;
- opportunity requires irreversible build before its central assumption can be tested;
- opportunity exists mainly because it is available;
- material ethical, reputational, authorship, data, confidentiality, or conflict risk.

For practice or venture opportunities, also apply the track-specific hard gates in `references/zero-to-one-management-practice.md`.

## Portfolio rules

Prioritise identity concentration and cumulative assets over activity count.

- **Platform-core:** can deepen the user's defining arena and produce cumulative assets.
- **Platform-adjacent:** adds useful scope, method, access, distribution, or option value without fragmenting identity.
- **Legacy/opportunistic:** legitimate but should not be mistaken for a strategic platform.

When comparing multiple opportunities, assess crowding, sequencing, complementarities, governance burden, and competition for the same high-value attention.

## Portfolio dispositions

Use one primary disposition. These are selection outcomes, not execution modes.

- **Core candidate:** deserves consideration for the user's defining portfolio; route to External Engagement or Adaptive Commitment according to the governing uncertainty.
- **Selective candidate:** strategically attractive but conditional; route to field evidence, partnership qualification, or bounded commitment according to the missing evidence.
- **Service-only:** legitimate duty, public contribution, or relationship maintenance without core platform status.
- **Monitor:** potentially attractive, but current evidence is insufficient for portfolio entry.
- **Redesign:** the underlying problem may be valuable, but the present opportunity architecture is flawed.
- **Reject:** no justified place in the portfolio under current conditions.

Do not use Own, Build, Option, Validate, Scale, Delegate, Park, or Kill as Strategic Opportunity dispositions. Those are execution or allocation decisions for Adaptive Commitment.

## Output standards

A full general evaluation should include:

- verdict and confidence;
- strategic score and platform classification;
- portfolio disposition;
- deeper arena and non-consensus insight;
- strongest reasons to proceed;
- strongest reasons to decline or redesign;
- triggered kill criteria;
- user's recommended role, control, and ownership position;
- reusable assets and portfolio effects;
- principal uncertainty and binding weakness;
- evidence that would raise or lower the verdict;
- realistic counterfactual comparison;
- current Agent state and evidence version;
- for re-screens, what changed and why;
- whether an External Engagement or Adaptive Commitment handoff is warranted;
- for systems-sensitive opportunities, the system diagnosis findings that materially raise, lower, or redesign the verdict;
- for heuristics-sensitive opportunities, the governing tension, binding constraint, concentration point, or strategic sequence that materially changes selection;
- for technology-possibility-sensitive opportunities, the changed action set, new bottleneck, capability window, interface position, or value-capture implication that materially changes selection.

For a management-practice or venture opportunity, add:

- Practice Impact score;
- Zero-to-One Venture score;
- original Seven Questions dashboard;
- hard-gate results and score caps;
- dual-track class: Build-to-Own Candidate, Impact Practice, Venture Hypothesis, or Redesign/Reject;
- source-bounded venture stress-test findings that materially change the judgement.

Do not include detailed resource floors, caps, protected time, first actions, milestones, persistence periods, review dates, or reallocation rules.

## Lightweight modes

For a quick pass, provide only:

- verdict;
- provisional strategic score;
- platform fit;
- portfolio disposition;
- main rationale;
- main risk;
- principal evidence gap;
- current state and evidence version;
- handoff destination or no handoff.

For portfolio review, group opportunities into:

- core candidates;
- selective candidates;
- service-only;
- monitor or redesign;
- reject.

## External Engagement handoff

Transfer:

1. current Agent state and evidence version;
2. opportunity definition and preliminary or updated disposition;
3. deeper arena and non-consensus insight;
4. principal uncertainty and decisive evidence gap;
5. current role, ownership, access, and governance evidence;
6. evidence that would raise, lower, or invalidate the verdict;
7. requested External Engagement mode: Opportunity Formation or Partnership Conversion;
8. next evidence-producing objective;
9. stop or downgrade rule.

External Engagement owns field learning and relationship structure. Strategic Opportunity retains authority over portfolio entry and re-screening.
