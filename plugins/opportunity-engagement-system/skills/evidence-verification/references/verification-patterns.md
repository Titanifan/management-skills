# Verification Patterns

Use these examples as patterns for claim decomposition and calibrated output. They are illustrative, not factual assertions about any current entity.

## Investment or company claim

Input:

"Company X has secured a second OEM licence."

Decompose:

- Company X has an arrangement with a second OEM.
- The arrangement qualifies as a licence rather than an MoU, supplier agreement, evaluation, qualification, or distribution relationship.
- The status is current as of the decision date.

Preferred evidence:

- exchange filing or formal company disclosure;
- issuing or counterpart evidence if available;
- independent corroboration when terminology is promotional or ambiguous.

Possible output:

- Status: Partially verified.
- Corrected wording: "Company X has announced a second OEM relationship; the available public evidence does not establish that the arrangement is a licence."
- Decision impact: Material if the investment thesis depends on repeat licensing validation.

## Policy or grant claim

Input:

"The call closes on 30 September and universities can apply without an industry partner."

Decompose:

- current closing date is 30 September in the relevant year and timezone;
- universities are eligible applicants;
- no industry partner is mandatory;
- no later amendment changes those conditions.

Preferred evidence:

- current official guidelines;
- program page;
- amendment or FAQ where legally or procedurally operative.

Do not rely on an old newsletter when current guidelines exist.

## Academic claim

Input:

"Prior research shows AI adoption improves firm performance."

Decompose:

- which form of AI adoption;
- which performance outcome;
- causal or associational claim;
- population and period;
- whether evidence comes from one study or a broader body of literature.

Possible correction:

"Several studies report a positive association between specified forms of AI adoption and selected performance outcomes, but the evidence provided here does not establish a general causal effect."

## Organisational-role claim

Input:

"Person Y currently leads the program."

Check:

- current official role page;
- effective date;
- acting versus substantive appointment;
- whether the named program still exists under the same structure.

Use archived pages only for historical status.

## Relationship claim

Input:

"The organisation is willing to fund the project."

Documentary evidence may establish:

- an email expressing interest;
- a formal letter of support;
- an approved budget;
- a signed funding agreement.

Do not collapse these into one state.

If only a conversation can determine actual willingness or authority, classify the documentary claim narrowly and return the field question to External Engagement.
