# Synthetic Evaluation Cases

## Purpose

Use these cases to test decision boundaries, provenance discipline, uncertainty handling, and handoffs. They do not prescribe a single correct verdict. An evaluator should judge whether the response uses the right Skill for the right decision and makes its reasoning auditable.

## Strategic Opportunity

### SO-01: AI course-planning product

**Prompt**

Should I pursue an AI course-planning tool for universities as a serious platform opportunity?

**Facts available at decision time**

- The user has research credibility in AI adoption and higher education but has not built software products.
- Two programme directors describe recurring timetable and curriculum problems.
- No buyer, procurement owner, or budget has been identified.
- A colleague proposes a grant application due in three weeks and wants the user to lead the conceptual work.
- A pilot could create a reusable decision model and dataset, but a production build would require external engineering and university data access.
- Several vendors already sell scheduling tools; the claimed difference is theory-informed curriculum design.

**Expected capabilities**

- Define the deeper arena, user role, platform fit, non-consensus mechanism, and realistic substitutes.
- Separate strategic attractiveness, practice impact, venture potential, evidence quality, and execution readiness.
- Apply hard gates before scores and distinguish discovery from creation uncertainty.
- Give a portfolio disposition and identify evidence that could change it.
- Hand resource floors, pilots, and milestones to Adaptive Commitment.

**Failure signals**

- Treating â€œAI in higher educationâ€ or a grant deadline as sufficient opportunity evidence.
- Assuming academic credibility supplies product, distribution, or ownership capability.
- Averaging away the absent buyer or data pathway.
- Producing a detailed build plan instead of a selection verdict.

### SO-02: High-profile policy advisory invitation

**Prompt**

A prestigious industry body has invited me to join an unpaid advisory group on green industrial policy. Is it strategically worth pursuing?

**Facts available at decision time**

- The topic fits the user's established research arena.
- Meetings are monthly, but members routinely produce briefing notes between meetings.
- The invitation offers visibility but no guaranteed attribution, data, publication, or direct government access.
- One senior member can introduce the user to a relevant regulator.
- The user is already carrying two high-governance projects.

**Expected capabilities**

- Distinguish arena fit from role quality, ownership, governance burden, and compounding assets.
- Compare core, selective, service-only, monitor, redesign, and reject dispositions without forcing a venture lens.
- Identify whether the invitation is an opportunity itself or an access route requiring External Engagement.
- State the evidence and conditions that would improve or weaken portfolio entry.

**Failure signals**

- Equating prestige or topic fit with strategic value.
- Treating unpaid public contribution as automatically worthless.
- Ignoring attribution, direct access, and existing governance load.
- Setting a time budget or persistence period before Adaptive Commitment review.

## External Engagement

### EE-01: Warm introduction to a mining company

**Prompt**

A university centre introduced me to a sustainability manager at a mining company. How should I turn this into a durable research and industry relationship?

**Facts available at decision time**

- The manager describes a persistent contractor-decarbonisation problem but does not control budget or data access.
- An operations executive owns the decision; procurement and legal functions can block data sharing.
- The centre expects acknowledgement for the introduction.
- The manager offers a second meeting and access to two operational staff but asks for a diagnostic presentation first.
- No scope, attribution, or reciprocal resource commitment exists yet.

**Expected capabilities**

- Preserve the centre's credit while building direct, attributable contact.
- Separate problem owner, sponsor, user, gatekeeper, and possible buyer.
- Design a bounded next conversation and reciprocal commitment test.
- State what may be scoped free and what would cross into delivery.
- Produce an External Engagement handoff without choosing the overall portfolio or full resource allocation.

**Failure signals**

- Calling the relationship owned after one introduction.
- Treating the enthusiastic manager as sponsor without evidence.
- Preparing a usable diagnostic before role, data, and commitment are clear.
- Recommending a grant as the first relationship move.
