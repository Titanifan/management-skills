# Synthetic Evaluation Cases

## Purpose

Use these cases to test decision boundaries, provenance discipline, uncertainty handling, and handoffs. They do not prescribe a single correct verdict. An evaluator should judge whether the response uses the right Skill for the right decision and makes its reasoning auditable.

## Strategic Opportunity

### SO-01: AI course-planning product

**Prompt**

Should I pursue an AI course-planning tool for universities as a serious platform opportunity?

**Facts available at decision time**

- The user has research credibility in AI adoption and higher education but has not built software products.
- Two programme directors describe recurring timetable and curriculum problems.
- No buyer, procurement owner, or budget has been identified.
- A colleague proposes a grant application due in three weeks and wants the user to lead the conceptual work.
- A pilot could create a reusable decision model and dataset, but a production build would require external engineering and university data access.
- Several vendors already sell scheduling tools; the claimed difference is theory-informed curriculum design.

**Expected capabilities**

- Define the deeper arena, user role, platform fit, non-consensus mechanism, and realistic substitutes.
- Separate strategic attractiveness, practice impact, venture potential, evidence quality, and execution readiness.
- Apply hard gates before scores and distinguish discovery from creation uncertainty.
- Give a portfolio disposition and identify evidence that could change it.
- Hand resource floors, pilots, and milestones to Adaptive Commitment.

**Failure signals**

- Treating “AI in higher education” or a grant deadline as sufficient opportunity evidence.
- Assuming academic credibility supplies product, distribution, or ownership capability.
- Averaging away the absent buyer or data pathway.
- Producing a detailed build plan instead of a selection verdict.

### SO-02: High-profile policy advisory invitation

**Prompt**

A prestigious industry body has invited me to join an unpaid advisory group on green industrial policy. Is it strategically worth pursuing?

**Facts available at decision time**

- The topic fits the user's established research arena.
- Meetings are monthly, but members routinely produce briefing notes between meetings.
- The invitation offers visibility but no guaranteed attribution, data, publication, or direct government access.
- One senior member can introduce the user to a relevant regulator.
- The user is already carrying two high-governance projects.

**Expected capabilities**

- Distinguish arena fit from role quality, ownership, governance burden, and compounding assets.
- Compare core, selective, service-only, monitor, redesign, and reject dispositions without forcing a venture lens.
- Identify whether the invitation is an opportunity itself or an access route requiring External Engagement.
- State the evidence and conditions that would improve or weaken portfolio entry.

**Failure signals**

- Equating prestige or topic fit with strategic value.
- Treating unpaid public contribution as automatically worthless.
- Ignoring attribution, direct access, and existing governance load.
- Setting a time budget or persistence period before Adaptive Commitment review.

## External Engagement

### EE-01: Warm introduction to a mining company

**Prompt**

A university centre introduced me to a sustainability manager at a mining company. How should I turn this into a durable research and industry relationship?

**Facts available at decision time**

- The manager describes a persistent contractor-decarbonisation problem but does not control budget or data access.
- An operations executive owns the decision; procurement and legal functions can block data sharing.
- The centre expects acknowledgement for the introduction.
- The manager offers a second meeting and access to two operational staff but asks for a diagnostic presentation first.
- No scope, attribution, or reciprocal resource commitment exists yet.

**Expected capabilities**

- Preserve the centre's credit while building direct, attributable contact.
- Separate problem owner, sponsor, user, gatekeeper, and possible buyer.
- Design a bounded next conversation and reciprocal commitment test.
- State what may be scoped free and what would cross into delivery.
- Produce an External Engagement handoff without choosing the overall portfolio or full resource allocation.

**Failure signals**

- Calling the relationship owned after one introduction.
- Treating the enthusiastic manager as sponsor without evidence.
- Preparing a usable diagnostic before role, data, and commitment are clear.
- Recommending a grant as the first relationship move.

### EE-02: Ghostwritten public submission

**Prompt**

An industry association wants me to write the technical core of its regulatory submission. It offers access to members but wants the submission under the association's name. How should I respond?

**Facts available at decision time**

- The consultation is strongly aligned with the user's expertise and public-interest agenda.
- The association has a clear policy position that may not match the user's independent analysis.
- No payment, attribution, publication right, or disclosure arrangement is offered.
- The regulator accepts independent expert submissions.
- The association can provide member data and introductions if governance is agreed.

**Expected capabilities**

- Distinguish honest brokerage, independent contribution, and private advocacy.
- Surface attribution, conflict, lobbying, data, and free-delivery boundaries.
- Identify redesign options and reciprocal commitments without treating payment as the only valid outcome.
- Preserve a possible public-policy relationship while refusing invisible or captured labour.

**Failure signals**

- Drafting the submission before resolving role and independence.
- Treating access promises as equivalent to reciprocal commitment.
- Assuming all unpaid policy work is service-only or all association work is illegitimate.
- Claiming policy impact before evidence of regulator use or follow-up.

## Adaptive Commitment

### AC-01: Two paid pilots, uncertain scale

**Prompt**

Two organisations have paid for pilots of my management diagnostic. How much should I commit now, and what would justify scaling it?

**Facts available at decision time**

- Both pilots produced useful feedback and one repeat request.
- Delivery still depends on the user's personal interviews and analysis.
- No standard onboarding, trained associate, or validated outcome measure exists.
- The user can protect one day per week for three months but is recovering from a demanding semester.
- A developer estimates six months for a software product, although the main adoption bottleneck may be workflow integration rather than technology.

**Expected capabilities**

- Accept the positive direction provisionally and focus on current commitment rather than re-running portfolio selection.
- Select a mode and stage, then state resource floor, cap, protected attention, owner, first action, and principal constraint.
- Define evidence, milestone, persistence period, review condition, and scale/redesign/park triggers.
- Treat capacity as a scope constraint without diagnosis.
- Separate pilot success from scale readiness and identify residual assets.

**Failure signals**

- Recommending full software build because two pilots paid.
- Leaving the project as an indefinite option without expiry or cap.
- Treating one repeat request as proof of a scalable business.
- Ignoring personal-delivery dependence and recovery capacity.

### AC-02: PhD rescue pressure

**Prompt**

My PhD student is behind, repeatedly submits weak drafts, and has an important milestone in ten weeks. How should I commit without abandoning my supervision duty or taking over the project?

**Facts available at decision time**

- The student attends meetings and works hard but implements feedback inconsistently.
- Prior written action plans were not completed.
- The research question remains viable, but the empirical design needs narrowing.
- The user has already rewritten several sections and feels responsible for the deadline.
- University milestone and support procedures are available.

**Expected capabilities**

- Separate supervision duty from strategic overinvestment and ghostwriting.
- Choose a bounded service-track or other justified mode with clear roles.
- Define the student's deliverable, evidence of progress, support floor, supervisor cap, review condition, and escalation pathway.
- Distinguish decision quality, execution fidelity, learning, and milestone outcome.
- Protect fairness, documentation, and developmental support.

**Failure signals**

- Advising the supervisor to rewrite the thesis to secure the milestone.
- Treating student effort as evidence of project progress.
- Killing a viable project without a bounded narrowing and support test.
- Giving generic encouragement without responsibility, evidence, and review rules.

## Evaluation rubric

For each case, rate the response on a 0–2 scale for:

1. correct decision boundary;
2. evidence versus inference discipline;
3. construct and mechanism use;
4. boundary conditions and failure modes;
5. non-mechanical treatment of scores or categories;
6. actionable handoff to the adjacent Skill.

Record disagreements and failure cases. Do not convert a high rubric total into `Case-calibrated` status without repeated independent runs and documented real-world cases.
