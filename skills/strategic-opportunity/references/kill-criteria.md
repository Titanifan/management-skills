# Kill Criteria

Apply these before detailed scoring. A hard kill should usually override an attractive score unless redesign removes the problem.

These are opportunity-selection kills. Do not use them to recreate execution management. Questions about resource caps, first actions, milestones, persistence, review dates, or reallocation belong to Adaptive Commitment.

## General hard kills

1. **No mechanism:** variables, context, or relevance exist, but no causal, process, or value-realisation mechanism can sustain the claim.
2. **No evidence pathway:** required evidence cannot realistically be obtained, cleaned, identified, validated, or defended.
3. **Commodity framing:** the topic and proposed contribution are interchangeable with crowded work.
4. **Identity dilution:** the opportunity weakens coherence unless explicitly classified as service-only.
5. **Weak role:** the user's contribution is peripheral, mainly labour, or lacks intellectual, execution, or ownership control.
6. **Effort-return mismatch:** the opportunity requires elite-level effort for weak strategic, scholarly, commercial, institutional, or public return.
7. **Fragile governance:** success depends on collaborators, students, customers, or partners without credible ownership, quality control, escalation, or accountability.
8. **No compounding asset:** completion would create no reusable theory, data, method, capability, relationships, funding position, distribution, workflow position, teaching material, public identity, deal flow, or ownership.
9. **Fashion trap:** attractiveness comes mainly from labels such as AI, ESG, sustainability, blockchain, critical minerals, or digital transformation rather than a distinctive problem.
10. **Irreversible-before-evidence architecture:** the opportunity requires major, difficult-to-reverse build before its central assumption can be credibly examined.
11. **Unbounded obligation:** responsibility can expand indefinitely through rescue work, emotional labour, administration, implementation, or custom consulting.
12. **Ethical or reputational risk:** avoidable authorship, data, participant, policy, conflict, confidentiality, institutional, or stakeholder risks are material.

## Practice-impact hard kills

Apply these when the opportunity claims practical impact:

- no specific decision owner, recurring process, or affected action;
- no plausible counterfactual decision effect;
- no path to repeated use or workflow integration;
- no legitimate access to users or affected stakeholders;
- no credible way to observe use, behavioural change, or outcomes;
- intervention creates material harm, surveillance, inequality, unsafe automation, regulatory gaming, or symbolic compliance without adequate safeguards.

## Zero-to-one venture hard kills

Apply these when the opportunity claims commercial scale, monopoly, or ownership:

- no defensible non-consensus insight or secret;
- no step-change over the strongest real substitute;
- no small, real starting market;
- no identifiable buyer, budget, or distribution route;
- artificial market definition that ignores actual substitutes;
- business remains structurally tied to unlimited bespoke labour;
- no compounding asset or durability mechanism;
- ownership, IP, data rights, control, or founder governance are structurally unavailable or misaligned;
- opportunity relies mainly on sector size, subsidy, social need, or hype;
- meaningful demand cannot be examined before a large irreversible build.

Use `zero-to-one-management-practice.md` for score caps and track-specific overrides.

## Soft warning signs

- The opportunity can be defended only as interesting, worthy, urgent, or available.
- The empirical context is replaceable without changing the argument.
- The contribution is another moderator, mediator, application, or dashboard without deeper leverage.
- The project adds activity but no reusable asset.
- Practice claims rely on dissemination, downloads, attendance, or praise rather than changed decisions.
- Venture claims rely on total addressable market rather than beachhead, buyer, and channel evidence.
- The user has little control over the core mechanism, data, customer relationship, or resulting asset.

## Redesign tests

Ask:

- Can the mechanism or value-realisation pathway be sharpened?
- Can the setting become theoretically or practically necessary?
- Can the user obtain ownership of a construct, dataset, method, capability, customer relationship, workflow, distribution position, or arena?
- Can the target be narrowed to a repeated high-value decision?
- Can the small starting market, buyer, or channel be specified?
- Can the central assumption be examined without a large irreversible build?
- Can scope and responsibility become structurally bounded?
- Can the project be integrated into an existing platform or portfolio?
- What evidence would materially change the selection verdict?

If redesign cannot remove the kill criterion, classify the opportunity as service-only, monitor, or reject. Leave execution decisions to Adaptive Commitment.
