# Strategic Opportunity Scoring Rubric

Use this 0-100 system for research projects, collaborations, grants, PhD topics, datasets, teaching initiatives, external engagements, and platform-building opportunities.

The score evaluates strategic attractiveness and portfolio fit. It does not determine resources, execution stage, persistence, or review rules. Apply hard kills first. Use Adaptive Commitment only after selection.

## Score summary

| Dimension | Weight | What it tests |
|---|---:|---|
| Non-consensus insight | 10 | Whether the opportunity contains a defensible insight that is not already obvious. |
| Arena potential | 10 | Whether it opens or deepens a cumulative arena rather than one isolated topic. |
| Institutional / policy friction | 10 | Whether meaningful institutional, regulatory, policy, nonmarket, or governance tension exists. |
| Theoretical leverage | 12 | Whether it can sharpen, extend, recombine, or challenge theory. |
| Data and method advantage | 10 | Whether evidence or analytical capability is credible and difficult to replicate. |
| Identity compounding | 12 | Whether it strengthens a recognisable scholarly or professional identity. |
| Defensibility / moat | 10 | Whether cumulative advantage becomes harder to copy over time. |
| Feasibility | 10 | Whether scope, role, capability, evidence, access, and governance are realistic. |
| Portfolio fit | 8 | Whether it complements the current portfolio without fragmentation. |
| Opportunity cost and downside risk | 8 | Whether expected strategic value justifies crowding and downside exposure. |

## Dimension anchors

### 1. Non-consensus insight, 0-10

- **High:** reveals an underrecognised mechanism, contradiction, or implication that is contestable but defensible.
- **Medium:** useful angle, mainly extending accepted arguments.
- **Low:** obvious, fashionable, descriptive, or interchangeable.

### 2. Arena potential, 0-10

- **High:** supports multiple outputs, datasets, grants, students, partners, methods, applications, or ownership pathways.
- **Medium:** supports one strong project with limited continuation.
- **Low:** one-off topic with no cumulative pathway.

### 3. Institutional / policy friction, 0-10

- **High:** the core problem depends on real institutional, regulatory, policy, legitimacy, nonmarket, geopolitical, human-rights, sustainability, or governance friction.
- **Medium:** context affects the project but remains secondary.
- **Low:** setting is generic and replaceable.

### 4. Theoretical leverage, 0-12

- **High:** changes explanation, constructs, mechanisms, boundary conditions, or integration across literatures.
- **Medium:** plausible contribution but under-specified.
- **Low:** topical, empirical, or managerial without theoretical architecture.

### 5. Data and method advantage, 0-10

- **High:** rare access, difficult dataset, credible identification, distinctive qualitative evidence, advanced analytics, or robust mixed methods.
- **Medium:** accessible evidence requiring careful construction.
- **Low:** crowded, weak, poorly matched, or unlikely to support the claim.

### 6. Identity compounding, 0-12

- **High:** clearly reinforces the user's chosen arena and future recognisability.
- **Medium:** compatible but peripheral.
- **Low:** creates a generic, fragmented, or contradictory profile.

### 7. Defensibility / moat, 0-10

- **High:** builds cumulative advantage through constructs, data, method, repeated context, policy expertise, partner access, student pipeline, reputation, distribution, workflow position, deal flow, or ownership.
- **Medium:** some advantage, but easy to copy.
- **Low:** competitors can reproduce it quickly.

### 8. Feasibility, 0-10

- **High:** scope, capability, role, evidence, access, collaborators, governance, and target are realistic.
- **Medium:** feasible only after narrowing, stronger access, or role redesign.
- **Low:** depends on fragile access, unclear ownership, excessive complexity, or unbounded claims.

### 9. Portfolio fit, 0-8

- **High:** complements core work, fills a real gap, and merits strategic consideration.
- **Medium:** compatible but not central.
- **Low:** increases queue congestion, context switching, or competition for the same cognitive capital.

### 10. Opportunity cost and downside risk, 0-8

- **High:** expected upside and residual assets justify the strategic crowding and downside.
- **Medium:** value is plausible but competes with stronger alternatives.
- **Low:** likely to crowd out higher-value work or create disproportionate governance, reputational, or attention costs.

## Portfolio disposition

- **85-100:** core candidate, subject to hard gates and evidence quality.
- **75-84:** selective candidate with explicit conditions.
- **65-74:** monitor or redesign; do not assume portfolio entry.
- **50-64:** service-only, monitor, or decline depending on duty and opportunity cost.
- **Below 50:** reject unless a radical redesign changes the opportunity.

## Evidence-quality override

Regardless of score, downgrade to provisional, monitor, or redesign when the user cannot specify:

- the principal uncertainty;
- a credible evidence pathway;
- the user's role and control;
- the main dependency or governance risk;
- what evidence would materially raise or lower the strategic verdict.

Do not prescribe a test, resource contract, milestone, or review date here. Transfer the evidence gap to Adaptive Commitment.
