# Zero-to-One Venture Stress Test

## Purpose and source boundary

Use this file only after a venture-oriented opportunity passes the rapid screen in `zero-to-one-management-practice.md`.

The seven categories—Engineering, Timing, Monopoly, People, Distribution, Durability, and Secret—are adapted from Peter Thiel and Blake Masters's *Zero to One* (2014). The questions below are a selective analytical adaptation for decision testing. They are not a chapter-by-chapter summary, a substitute for the book, or a peer-reviewed predictive model.

Strategic-entrepreneurship research can explain why opportunity-seeking and advantage-seeking belong together; it does not validate this practitioner framework. See `provenance.md` for the source boundary.

## How to use the test

1. Start with the seven-question dashboard in `zero-to-one-management-practice.md`.
2. Apply the two adversarial tests under each weak, uncertain, or suspiciously strong rating.
3. Apply all four cross-cutting tests.
4. Change a score, cap, classification, or verdict only when the test exposes material evidence.
5. Report the tests that changed the judgement; do not dump every question into the output.

## Seven-category adversarial test

### Engineering

- Against the strongest real substitute—including manual work, an incumbent, internal provision, and inaction—what observable result would constitute a step-change rather than polish or incremental improvement?
- Which integrated capability, proprietary asset, or difficult-to-reproduce mechanism creates that difference, and what evidence would falsify the claim?

### Timing

- Which technological, regulatory, institutional, cost, or behavioural change makes the opportunity unusually possible now rather than merely fashionable now?
- Are the necessary complements—data, infrastructure, skills, standards, financing, procurement, and legitimacy—ready, and would the opportunity survive if hype, subsidies, or easy capital weakened?

### Monopoly

- What is the smallest real beachhead defined by shared needs, buying behaviour, and reachable distribution, and what broader substitutes reveal whether the market definition is self-serving?
- If the opportunity succeeds, which mechanism permits value capture and continued differentiation instead of commodity competition or appropriation by a platform, supplier, customer, or institution?

### People

- Do the core participants collectively possess or credibly acquire the technical, domain, distribution, operating, and governance capabilities, with explicit ownership and decision rights?
- Which founder or key-person trait is simultaneously an advantage and a governance risk, and what can become institutional capability before dependence becomes dangerous?

### Distribution

- Who uses, buys, controls the budget, approves, blocks, implements, and benefits—and does the transaction value support the required acquisition, onboarding, service, and retention effort?
- Is adoption designed into product and workflow, or does every new customer require equivalent founder persuasion and bespoke labour?

### Durability

- Which asset or position compounds with every user, case, transaction, or implementation: proprietary technology, data, network, scale, brand, workflow position, learning, or distribution?
- What technical, policy, platform, geopolitical, substitution, succession, or business-model change could erase the advantage, and can value detach from the founder's personal labour before then?

### Secret

- What important, valuable, actionable, and falsifiable claim do capable actors overlook or reject, beyond a broad trend or contrarian slogan?
- Why have competent incumbents not acted: missing evidence, incentives, status, fragmented ownership, regulation, coordination failure, or because the claimed secret is false?

## Cross-cutting tests

### Market truth

Name the strongest substitute competing for the same budget, attention, trust, or workflow position. If the opportunity is unique only after excluding that substitute, reduce the venture claim.

### Public value and legitimacy

Identify who benefits, who bears risk, and whether monopoly, secrecy, concentration, or private value capture conflicts with the opportunity's public purpose. Preserve a high-impact opportunity through the PIP track when ZVP assumptions are inappropriate.

### Ownership, possession, and control

State who economically owns the asset, who operates it, and who can make or reverse the decisive choices. If the user cannot preserve meaningful ownership or control, classify the path honestly as impact practice or service rather than build-to-own.

### Non-linear upside versus labour dependence

Ask whether full success creates a compounding outcome or merely more paid work. A profitable, respected, or socially valuable service can remain worth pursuing, but it is not a zero-to-one venture when growth remains linearly tied to personal hours.

## Interpretation discipline

- Do not count the same evidence under several categories without explaining the shared mechanism.
- Do not reward sector importance, growth, subsidies, technology fashion, or social need without a micro-level value-realisation path.
- Do not treat five or six superficial yes answers as a pass.
- Do not force public-interest, scholarly, or service opportunities to satisfy a monopoly-oriented lens.
- Hand execution mode, resource allocation, milestones, and review conditions to Adaptive Commitment.
