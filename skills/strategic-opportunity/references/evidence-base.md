# Evidence Base for Strategic Opportunity

## Purpose and limits

Use this file to trace the main constructs and mechanisms behind Strategic Opportunity. It is a provenance map, not a claim that the cited literature directly validates the complete workflow, score weights, portfolio dispositions, or its application to an individual academic career.

Most sources theorise firms, organisational units, or corporate managers. Translating them to a scholar's opportunity portfolio is an explicit cross-level inference. Treat the scoring rubric as a disciplined elicitation device rather than an empirically estimated model.

## Construct-to-rule map

| Construct or mechanism | Evidence provenance | Workflow translation | Boundary condition or failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strategy as an evolving pattern | Mintzberg (1978); Mintzberg and Waters (1985) | Evaluate the emerging stream of commitments and assets, not only the stated plan. Allow redesign when realised direction diverges from intent. | Retrospective pattern recognition does not by itself tell the user which opportunity to choose ex ante. |
| Exploration versus exploitation | March (1991) | Treat opportunity selection as a portfolio problem: protect some exploration without allowing every uncertain idea to claim core status. | The original model concerns organisational learning. A fixed universal balance is not implied. |
| Resource heterogeneity and defensibility | Barney (1991) | Ask whether an opportunity builds valuable, scarce, difficult-to-copy, and non-substitutable assets rather than activity alone. | Resource attributes are context-dependent; a personal reputation or relationship is not automatically a defensible firm resource. |
| Attention structures | Ocasio (1997) | Make cognitive capital and attention crowding explicit selection costs. Examine which issues and answers the opportunity will repeatedly place before the user. | Attention is shaped by organisational rules and relationships as well as individual choice. The user cannot always reallocate it unilaterally. |
| Behavioural bounds and cognitively distant opportunities | Gavetti (2012) | Require a non-consensus mechanism and ask whether the opportunity is overlooked for a reason the user can overcome. | Novelty is not evidence of value. Cognitively distant opportunities can also be implausible or inaccessible. |
| Dynamic managerial capabilities | Adner and Helfat (2003) | Assess whether the user's cognition, human capital, and social capital make them unusually able to act in the arena. | Managerial heterogeneity does not guarantee superior outcomes, and the evidence is at the corporate level. |
| Emergent internal ventures and strategic context | Burgelman (1983) | Distinguish a promising local initiative from an opportunity that can secure strategic context, ownership, and resources. | Evidence from internal corporate venturing may not transfer to independent ventures, public policy, or academic work without adaptation. |
| Sequential investment under uncertainty | McGrath (1997) | Prefer opportunities whose central uncertainty can be tested before irreversible build; preserve option value when uncertainty is high. | Calling an activity an option does not make it cheap, reversible, or informative. Options require an exercise logic and an expiry condition. |
| Zero-to-one venture questions | Thiel and Masters (2014) | Use the practitioner framework only as an adversarial venture module covering novelty, distribution, durability, team, and value capture. | This is a practitioner book, not peer-reviewed validation. Its monopoly-oriented assumptions should not govern public-interest or non-commercial work. |

## Derived decision rules

The evidence supports five broad translations:

1. Judge opportunities as paths that accumulate commitments and assets, not as isolated events.
2. Separate exploration value from a claim to core portfolio status.
3. Ask what becomes more valuable, defensible, or accessible after repeated action.
4. Treat attention, ownership, and organisational context as causal constraints rather than implementation details.
5. Use staged tests when uncertainty is high and irreversibility can be delayed.

These translations do not justify the exact 0-100 weights, thresholds, or labels in the Skill. Those are authorial design choices that require calibration against cases.

## Calibration cautions

- Do not convert citations into an authority claim for a specific verdict.
- Do not infer causality from conceptual or process research.
- Do not treat firm-level constructs as automatically valid at the individual level.
- Do not reward novelty without a value-realisation pathway.
- Do not let option language legitimise indefinite low-commitment activity.
- Record cases where a high-quality decision produces a poor outcome and where luck rescues a weak decision.
