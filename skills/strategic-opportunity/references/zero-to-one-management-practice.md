## 1. Purpose and source boundary

Use this module to evaluate a management-practice opportunity along two separate tracks:

- **Practice Impact Potential (PIP):** whether the opportunity can change a real, repeated, high-value decision or organisational process.
- **Zero-to-One Venture Potential (ZVP):** whether the opportunity can become distinctive, scalable, defensible, durable, distributable, and economically ownable.

Do not collapse these tracks into one score. A policy submission, regulatory diagnostic, public-interest framework, or executive intervention may have high practice impact without venture potential. A venture concept may have high theoretical upside before it has demonstrated any real decision effect.

The ZVP track preserves Peter Thiel and Blake Masters's original seven business questions as the core dashboard:

1. Engineering
2. Timing
3. Monopoly
4. People
5. Distribution
6. Durability
7. Secret

Use `zero-to-one-question-bank.md` for a lean, source-bounded adversarial test of those seven categories. It is an analytical adaptation, not a reproduction or substitute for the book. The PIP track, weights, caps, thresholds, and dual-track classification are `Authorial-synthesis`; do not attribute them to the book. Read `provenance.md` when auditing these distinctions.

## 2. When to load this module

Load this module when the opportunity involves any of the following:

- theory-to-method translation;
- consulting diagnostic or advisory method;
- executive education or professional training;
- management decision tool or governance framework;
- policy, regulatory, or public-investment diagnostic;
- industry research platform;
- dataset, dashboard, AI product, analytics tool, or workflow system;
- commercialisation of academic knowledge;
- venture, spin-out, investment-screening method, or ownership pathway;
- repeated industry engagement intended to create practical or economic value.

Do not require ZVP strength for purely scholarly opportunities unless the user explicitly wants commercialisation or ownership.

## 3. Two-stage assessment architecture

### Stage 1: rapid dual-track screen

Use a 10-15 minute screen to determine whether the opportunity merits deeper work. Assess practice viability and venture viability separately. A failure on one track does not automatically kill the other.

### Stage 2: full assessment

Run two scores:

- PIP: 0-100 across eight dimensions.
- ZVP: 0-100 across the original seven questions, challenged by the lean venture stress test.

Then classify the opportunity in a two-by-two matrix. Do not average PIP and ZVP.

## 4. Common scoring anchor

Rate each dimension from 0 to 4.

| Rating | Meaning | Evidence standard |
|---:|---|---|
| 0 | Absent or contradicted | Evidence points against the claim. |
| 1 | Weak or rhetorical | Mainly aspiration, analogy, or sector narrative. |
| 2 | Plausible but unvalidated | Coherent hypothesis with limited evidence. |
| 3 | Credibly evidenced | Concrete cases, users, data, or demonstrated mechanisms. |
| 4 | Repeatedly validated and compounding | Repeated use, observed decision effects, paying demand, proprietary learning, or strengthening advantage. |

Calculate each track as:

`track score = sum(dimension weight x rating / 4)`

Use judgement rather than mechanical arithmetic when evidence quality differs sharply. State major uncertainty and score caps.

## 5. Stage 1: rapid dual-track screen

### 5.1 Practice viability gates

Score each item 0, 1, or 2:

- 0 = no;
- 1 = uncertain or partial;
- 2 = yes with concrete evidence.

| Gate | Rapid question |
|---|---|
| P1. Decision owner | Is there a specific role, committee, organisation, or community that owns the decision or process? |
| P2. Repeated stakes | Does the decision recur, and is error costly in money, time, safety, legitimacy, capability, or public value? |
| P3. Decision effect | Could the method change an allocation, selection, design, governance, scale, exit, or implementation decision? |
| P4. Usability | Can it become a repeatable artefact, rule, workflow, protocol, dataset, or intervention rather than only an article or presentation? |
| P5. Evidence pathway | Can use and effects be observed within a credible period? |

Interpretation:

- **8-10:** pass to full PIP assessment.
- **5-7:** redesign or classify as provisional; transfer the evidence gap to Adaptive Commitment if the opportunity remains strategically relevant.
- **0-4:** do not claim practice impact; classify as service-only, redesign, monitor, or reject unless the opportunity is scholarly for other reasons.

### 5.2 Venture viability gates

Score each item 0, 1, or 2.

| Gate | Rapid question |
|---|---|
| V1. Secret | Is there a valuable, defensible non-consensus insight rather than a fashionable topic? |
| V2. Step-change | Could the offer be materially better than existing alternatives, not merely more polished? |
| V3. Beachhead | Is there a small, real starting market or user group that could be dominated? |
| V4. Buyer and distribution | Is there an identifiable buyer, budget, or reliable route to adoption? |
| V5. Compounding asset | Will use create proprietary data, workflow position, network, IP, reputation, learning, deal flow, or other cumulative advantage? |
| V6. Team and control | Can a credible team obtain the required technical, domain, sales, and governance capabilities with aligned ownership and control? |
| V7. Durability and ownership | Can the opportunity become less dependent on personal labour and preserve economic or strategic ownership over time? |

Interpretation:

- **11-14:** pass to full ZVP assessment.
- **7-10:** venture hypothesis; identify the binding uncertainty and defer any validation design to Adaptive Commitment.
- **0-6:** treat as an impact practice, service-only opportunity, or non-venture opportunity unless redesigned.

### 5.3 Stage 1 hard-stop conditions

Stop or radically redesign the relevant track when any of these is clearly true:

- no real decision owner, user, beneficiary, or buyer;
- no recognised problem or decision consequence;
- no evidence pathway;
- value depends entirely on a broad macro trend or social need;
- the offer is generic consulting, generic AI, generic ESG, or another commodity label;
- implementation requires unlimited bespoke labour;
- the claimed market exists only through artificial redefinition;
- there is no plausible route to value capture or ownership;
- material ethical, institutional, data, confidentiality, or conflict problems cannot be contained.

## 6. Stage 2A: Practice Impact Potential

Score the following eight dimensions.

| Dimension | Weight | Core test |
|---|---:|---|
| 1. Decision specificity and ownership | 15 | Is the target decision concrete, and is there a named decision owner? |
| 2. Recurrence and stakes | 15 | Is it repeated and materially consequential? |
| 3. Decision effect and counterfactual | 15 | Can the intervention plausibly change what is selected, funded, governed, implemented, scaled, stopped, or learned? |
| 4. Workflow integration and repeated use | 15 | Can it enter an existing routine, committee, stage gate, budget cycle, reporting process, or operating system? |
| 5. Evidence and observability | 10 | Can use, behavioural change, and outcomes be measured or credibly traced? |
| 6. Adoption and access | 10 | Can the user reach decision makers and secure legitimate adoption? |
| 7. Transferability and delivery burden | 10 | Can the method travel across cases without unlimited custom labour? |
| 8. Responsible and legitimate impact | 10 | Are affected stakeholders, downside, ethics, legitimacy, and distributional consequences handled? |
| **Total** | **100** |  |

### 6.1 Practice Impact question prompts

#### Decision specificity and ownership

- What exact decision or recurring process changes?
- Who owns it, approves it, uses it, and is accountable for the result?
- Is the target a decision rather than a vague aspiration such as awareness, transformation, or better strategy?
- Is the user's role sufficiently central to influence design or use?

#### Recurrence and stakes

- How often does the decision occur?
- What is the cost of a wrong decision or delayed action?
- Is the problem important to the decision owner, or mainly interesting to the researcher?
- Does the opportunity address a bottleneck rather than an incidental inconvenience?

#### Decision effect and counterfactual

- What would the decision maker do without this intervention?
- What could change: ranking, resource allocation, timing, conditions, governance, implementation, scale, or exit?
- Can one identify a decision that should differ after use?
- Is the method advisory only, or can it alter the choice architecture?

#### Workflow integration and repeated use

- Where in the workflow will the method sit?
- Who supplies inputs, interprets outputs, challenges the result, and acts?
- What event triggers use?
- Can it be used repeatedly without the creator present?
- Does repeated use improve the method or merely repeat delivery effort?

#### Evidence and observability

- What leading indicators show decision change?
- What later outcomes show value or harm?
- Can attribution be supported through before-after comparison, matched cases, process tracing, user logs, or decision records?
- Is the evaluation period realistic?

#### Adoption and access

- Is there a credible path to the decision owner?
- What incentives, status, trust, or legitimacy support adoption?
- Who may resist, and why?
- Does adoption require procurement, legal, IT, ethics, union, regulatory, or community approval?

#### Transferability and delivery burden

- Which elements are general and which are context-specific?
- Can the method be standardised without losing validity?
- What training or interpretation is required?
- Does each new case create assets, or only more consulting hours?

#### Responsible and legitimate impact

- Who benefits and who bears risk?
- Could the intervention intensify surveillance, inequality, unsafe automation, displacement, regulatory gaming, or symbolic compliance?
- Are human review, appeal, audit, and accountability mechanisms appropriate?
- Is public or social value real rather than rhetorical?

### 6.2 PIP interpretation

- **85-100:** demonstrated or highly credible practice platform; repeated use and impact evidence should exist or be imminent.
- **75-84:** strong practice opportunity; evidence on integration and use would strengthen the case.
- **60-74:** credible but incomplete; identify the adoption, workflow, or outcome evidence still required.
- **40-59:** engagement or advisory potential, but practice impact remains unproven.
- **Below 40:** weak practice claim; redesign around a real decision or stop presenting it as impact.

## 7. Stage 2B: Zero-to-One Venture Potential

The seven original questions form the scored dashboard. Use the source-bounded stress test to challenge each rating and expose self-deception.

| Original question | Weight | What to test |
|---|---:|---|
| 1. Engineering | 16 | Step-change value, vertical progress, integrated design, and human-technology complementarity. |
| 2. Timing | 10 | Why now, historical window, enabling conditions, and sequencing. |
| 3. Monopoly | 16 | Honest market definition, beachhead dominance, value capture, and escape from commodity competition. |
| 4. People | 12 | Founder and team fit, foundations, culture, governance, ownership, possession, and control. |
| 5. Distribution | 14 | Buyer, channel, sales economics, adoption, and repeatable delivery. |
| 6. Durability | 14 | Last-mover position, monopoly mechanisms, compounding assets, definite plan, and succession. |
| 7. Secret | 18 | Valuable non-consensus truth, natural or human secret, and unique opportunity. |
| **Total** | **100** |  |

### 7.1 Engineering

Ask:

- Does the offer create a qualitative step-change rather than incremental improvement?
- Can superiority be made obvious to the user despite switching cost and implementation risk?
- Does it solve a previously unsolved coordination, information, capability, or transaction problem?
- Is the improvement based on proprietary technology, data, integrated design, method, or workflow rather than presentation quality?
- Where relevant, do humans and technology complement each other in a way neither could achieve alone?
- Is the claimed improvement measured against the real alternative, including manual work, incumbent software, internal teams, and doing nothing?

### 7.2 Timing

Ask:

- Why is this possible or necessary now rather than five years earlier or later?
- Which technological, regulatory, institutional, market, cost, cultural, or distribution changes create the window?
- Is the opportunity early enough to shape the category but mature enough for adoption?
- Are enabling complements available: data, infrastructure, skills, standards, financing, procurement, or legitimacy?
- Is the plan exploiting a genuine one-time window or merely following hype?
- If the market moves slowly, is there a definite plan to enter and take it over?

### 7.3 Monopoly

Ask:

- What is the smallest real market in which the opportunity could become the default or dominant solution?
- Is that market defined by actual substitutability and buying behaviour rather than rhetorical narrowing?
- Does the offer create value and capture enough of it to finance continued improvement?
- Can it escape direct price competition and undifferentiated service delivery?
- What adjacent market follows only after beachhead dominance?
- Is the opportunity trying to disrupt a powerful incumbent unnecessarily, or can it establish a new category or complementary position?
- Does growth strengthen rather than dilute differentiation?

### 7.4 People

Ask:

- Do the founders or core collaborators have shared history, trust, and complementary capabilities?
- Are technical, domain, product, distribution, and governance needs covered?
- Are ownership, day-to-day possession, and ultimate control aligned?
- Is the board or decision structure small and functional?
- Are incentives long-term and tied to value creation rather than status or short-term cash extraction?
- Why would an exceptional later hire join this team instead of a safer or better-paid alternative?
- Does each key person have a clear primary responsibility?
- Can governance contain founder extremes without destroying the distinctive vision?

### 7.5 Distribution

Ask:

- Who is the user, buyer, budget owner, approver, blocker, and beneficiary?
- Which single channel is most likely to dominate acquisition and adoption?
- Does the transaction value support the required sales process?
- Is the offer trapped in a distribution dead zone: too expensive for self-service, too cheap for high-touch sales?
- Can customer lifetime value exceed acquisition, onboarding, support, and implementation costs?
- Is distribution designed into the product and workflow rather than added after creation?
- Can the founder or team sell the mission to employees, partners, investors, and customers?
- Can use become viral, referential, mandated, embedded, or otherwise self-reinforcing?

### 7.6 Durability

Ask:

- Why should this position remain valuable in 10 or 20 years?
- Which monopoly mechanisms apply: proprietary technology, network effects, scale economies, or brand?
- Does every customer, case, or transaction improve the data, method, distribution, workflow position, or economics?
- What technical, policy, platform, geopolitical, or business-model change could destroy the advantage?
- Can the opportunity become the last major mover in its category rather than merely the first entrant?
- Is there a definite long-term plan and sequence, not only a portfolio of options?
- Can the organisation institutionalise knowledge and survive founder transition?
- Does value eventually detach from personal billable labour?

### 7.7 Secret

Ask:

- What important and valuable claim is not widely believed?
- What does the relevant industry assume that may be false?
- Is the secret about nature or technology, or about people, incentives, institutions, and behaviour?
- Why have capable incumbents, researchers, or investors not acted on it?
- Is it hidden by disciplinary boundaries, status, taboo, regulation, fragmented data, coordination failure, or incompatible incentives?
- What evidence could falsify the secret?
- Does the secret lead to a specific product, method, market, or decision advantage?
- Who should know it, and what must remain proprietary?

### 7.8 ZVP interpretation

- **85-100:** exceptional zero-to-one architecture; rare and should require strong evidence on most dimensions.
- **75-84:** strong venture architecture with a credible secret, distribution path, and compounding advantage.
- **60-74:** promising venture hypothesis; identify the binding weakness and hand execution design to Adaptive Commitment.
- **40-59:** useful service or ordinary business; redesign if scalable ownership is the goal.
- **Below 40:** not a zero-to-one venture under current design.

Do not award an exceptional score simply because the sector is important, growing, subsidised, technologically fashionable, or socially valuable.

## 8. Hard gates, score caps, and overrides

### 8.1 Practice score caps

Apply these caps unless contrary evidence is strong:

- No real decision case examined: PIP maximum 59.
- No observed change in decision or behaviour: Decision Effect rating maximum 2.
- No repeated use: Workflow Integration rating maximum 2 and PIP maximum 74.
- No identifiable decision owner: Decision Specificity rating maximum 1.
- No legitimate access or adoption path: Adoption rating maximum 1.
- No credible measurement or tracing: Evidence rating maximum 1.

### 8.2 Venture score caps

- No specific starting market: Monopoly rating maximum 1.
- No identifiable buyer or distribution path: Distribution rating maximum 1 and ZVP maximum 59.
- No validated customer or user problem: ZVP maximum 59.
- No use, pilot, or demand evidence: ZVP maximum 69.
- No paying or institutionally committed user where payment is relevant: Distribution rating maximum 2 and ZVP maximum 74.
- No proprietary or compounding asset: Durability rating maximum 2.
- No defensible non-consensus insight: Secret rating maximum 1 and ZVP maximum 59.
- No plausible step-change: Engineering rating maximum 2.
- No credible team or capability acquisition plan: People rating maximum 2.
- Ownership or control is structurally unavailable: classify as service-only or Impact Practice, not Build-to-Own Candidate.
- Revenue remains linearly tied to the user's personal hours: ZVP maximum 59 unless a credible productisation path exists.

### 8.3 Power-law override

Ask whether full success could produce a result disproportionately larger than ordinary alternatives. If the upside remains linear, local, and labour-bound, do not classify the opportunity as a zero-to-one venture even if it is profitable.

### 8.4 Market-truth override

Reject market definitions that hide actual substitutes. Ask what the customer would do instead and which alternatives compete for the same budget, attention, trust, or workflow position.

### 8.5 Definite-plan override

A venture can remain exploratory, but it must articulate a concrete desired future and a sequence of milestones. Iteration should test a plan, not substitute for one.

### 8.6 Foundations override

Treat serious founder, collaborator, equity, IP, governance, or control misalignment as a hard gate. Early structural defects are unusually persistent.

## 9. Dual-track classification

Use 70 as the default threshold for high potential on each track. Adjust only with explicit reasoning.

| Practice Impact | Venture Potential | Classification | Default interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| High | High | **Build-to-Own Candidate** | A real decision problem with credible scale, moat, distribution, compounding, and ownership potential. This is an opportunity classification, not an instruction to build. |
| High | Low | **Impact Practice** | Valuable policy, professional, educational, or organisational intervention with limited monopoly or ownership potential. |
| Low | High | **Venture Hypothesis** | Attractive architecture or upside, but the real problem, user, workflow, or effect remains unvalidated. Adaptive Commitment must resolve whether any validation commitment is justified. |
| Low | Low | **Redesign or Reject** | No strong impact or zero-to-one case under current design. A legitimate duty may still be classified as service-only by the general Strategic Opportunity assessment. |

When scores are between 60 and 69, describe the opportunity as emerging rather than force a binary classification.

## 10. Evidence and calibration rules

1. Distinguish claim, inference, and observed evidence.
2. Use actual decisions, users, buyers, budgets, workflows, and outcomes wherever possible.
3. Penalise answers based mainly on sector growth, social need, government attention, or technology hype.
4. Do not allow the same evidence to inflate several dimensions without explanation.
5. Test against the strongest real substitute, including inaction and internal provision.
6. State what evidence would raise or lower each weak rating.
7. Re-score after problem interviews, pilots, repeated use, payment, workflow integration, and outcome observation.
8. Keep a decision log so later success or failure can update the rubric rather than merely confirm hindsight.
9. Use the book-derived criteria as a demanding venture lens, not as universal moral truth. They systematically favour concentration, monopoly, private value capture, and founder control.
10. Preserve public-interest opportunities through the separate PIP track.

## 11. Full workflow

1. Define the management practice in one sentence.
2. Name the exact decision owner, decision, recurrence, and stakes.
3. Identify the user, buyer, budget, beneficiary, and blockers.
4. State the non-consensus insight and value-realisation mechanism.
5. Run Stage 1 practice and venture gates separately.
6. Apply track-specific hard stops.
7. If practice passes, score PIP.
8. If venture passes or remains a serious hypothesis, score the original seven questions.
9. Load `zero-to-one-question-bank.md` and apply only the adversarial tests that could change a score, cap, or verdict.
10. Apply caps and overrides.
11. Place the opportunity in the dual-track matrix.
12. Compare the result with the general Strategic Opportunity score and platform fit.
13. State the binding constraint, principal uncertainty, and evidence needed to change the selection verdict.
14. Hand positive or provisional opportunities to Adaptive Commitment for execution mode, stage, resources, validation design, and review rules.

## 13. Handoff to Adaptive Commitment

Do not create a full commitment contract inside Strategic Opportunity. Transfer the following:

- portfolio disposition;
- Zero-to-One class, when applicable;
- current evidence stage;
- principal uncertainty;
- binding constraint;
- evidence that would strengthen or weaken the opportunity case;
- expected residual assets and governance constraints.

Adaptive Commitment should then determine execution mode and stage, resource floor and cap, protected time, responsible owner, first action, milestone, persistence period, review condition, and reallocation rules.
