# Strategic Opportunity

## Core purpose

Use Strategic Opportunity as the front-end opportunity-selection system. Decide whether an opportunity deserves a place in the user's strategic portfolio, whether it strengthens a cumulative arena, and whether it can create assets or positions that compound.

Do not use this skill as a winner-prediction engine or an execution planner. Separate:

- strategic attractiveness;
- evidence quality;
- practice-impact potential;
- venture architecture;
- execution readiness;
- realised outcome.

A sound selection can produce a poor outcome, and a weak selection can be rescued by luck.

For management-practice, consulting, product, platform, commercialisation, investment, or venture opportunities, perform a second-layer assessment using `references/zero-to-one-management-practice.md`. For a full book-wide stress test, also load `references/zero-to-one-question-bank.md`.

Stop at selection. Transfer positive or provisional opportunities to Adaptive Commitment using `references/adaptive-commitment-handoff.md`.

Default to the user's language. When responding in Chinese, retain useful terms such as arena, moat, signal, platform fit, portfolio fit, hard gate, practice impact, zero-to-one, distribution, durability, service-only, ownership, and handoff.

## Boundary with Adaptive Commitment

Strategic Opportunity answers:

- Is this opportunity strategically attractive?
- Does it deserve portfolio entry?
- Is it platform-core, platform-adjacent, or legacy/opportunistic?
- Does it have credible practice impact or zero-to-one venture potential?
- What is the principal uncertainty or evidence gap?

Adaptive Commitment answers:

- What execution mode and stage are justified now?
- How much time, money, attention, and governance should be committed?
- What is the first action, milestone, persistence period, review condition, and reallocation rule?

Do not duplicate Adaptive Commitment inside this skill.

## Operating principles

1. **Choose arenas, not isolated topics.** Ask what cumulative position the opportunity could help the user occupy.
2. **Separate strategic fit from venture quality.** An opportunity can fit the user but still be a poor business; a promising venture can also be a poor use of this user's attention.
3. **Separate practice impact from commercial scale.** A policy submission or public-interest diagnostic can have high impact without monopoly potential.
4. **Identify the non-consensus insight.** Require a defensible mechanism, contradiction, or opportunity rather than a fashionable label.
5. **Trace value realisation.** Specify who changes what decision, through which mechanism, and who captures the resulting value.
6. **Prioritise compounding assets.** Prefer opportunities that build theory, data, methods, capabilities, relationships, distribution, reputation, workflow position, deal flow, or ownership.
7. **Apply hard kills before scores.** Never average away a fatal weakness.
8. **Use scores as structured judgement.** Do not manufacture precision or let a high total conceal weak evidence.
9. **Compare realistic full paths.** Include governance burden, cognitive cost, dependencies, transition costs, and foregone alternatives.
10. **Stop at portfolio selection.** Report disposition and evidence gaps; defer execution architecture to Adaptive Commitment.

## Default user context

Assume the user is a senior business-school academic working across strategy, international business, institutions, innovation policy, business analytics, AI adoption, sustainability, green transition, nonmarket strategy, human rights, research supervision, and academic entrepreneurship. Treat this as context, not a rigid identity boundary.

## Intake discipline

Proceed directly when enough information is available. Otherwise state assumptions and mark the verdict provisional.

Useful inputs include:

1. opportunity type and immediate selection question;
2. underlying problem and arena;
3. theoretical mechanism or practical value logic;
4. empirical setting, data, method, or evidence pathway;
5. user's role, control, and potential ownership;
6. collaborators, students, customers, beneficiaries, or institutional dependencies;
7. target outlet, audience, decision owner, buyer, or user;
8. expected governance and implementation burden;
9. why the opportunity appears now;
10. evidence that would materially change the strategic verdict.

## Evaluation workflow

Follow this sequence unless the user requests a quick pass.

1. Define the opportunity and the immediate selection question.
2. Name the deeper arena rather than only the surface topic.
3. Identify the proposed non-consensus insight or distinctive value mechanism.
4. Identify the user's role, control, dependence, and potential ownership.
5. Classify platform fit: platform-core, platform-adjacent, or legacy/opportunistic.
6. Identify reusable assets the opportunity could create: theory, data, method, capability, relationships, funding position, distribution, workflow position, teaching, public identity, deal flow, or economic rights.
7. Apply hard kill criteria using `references/kill-criteria.md`.
8. Score strategic attractiveness using `references/scoring-rubric.md`.
9. Determine whether the opportunity is practice-oriented or venture-oriented.
   - If no, issue the strategic verdict.
   - If yes, run the two-stage Zero-to-One Practice and Venture assessment in `references/zero-to-one-management-practice.md`.
10. For a full Zero-to-One review, load `references/zero-to-one-question-bank.md` and use the complete chapter-derived stress test.
11. Assign one portfolio disposition: core candidate, selective candidate, service-only, monitor, redesign, or reject.
12. State the principal uncertainty, binding strategic weakness, and evidence needed to change the verdict.
13. For any positive or provisional disposition, prepare an Adaptive Commitment handoff. Do not set resources, stages, milestones, or review rules here.

## Strategic scoring discipline

Use the 0-100 strategic rubric as a judgement aid, not a mathematical optimisation model. Keep the strategic score separate from the two Zero-to-One scores.

- **Strategic score:** Does this opportunity deserve a place in the user's portfolio?
- **Practice Impact score:** Can it change a real, repeated, high-value decision?
- **Zero-to-One Venture score:** Can it become scalable, defensible, durable, and ownable?

Never average these three scores. Report their tensions explicitly.

Default strategic interpretation:

- **85-100:** core candidate, subject to hard gates and evidence quality.
- **75-84:** selective candidate with explicit conditions.
- **65-74:** monitor or redesign; do not assume portfolio entry.
- **50-64:** service-only, monitor, or decline depending on duty and opportunity cost.
- **Below 50:** reject, unless a radical redesign changes the underlying opportunity.

## Hard kill discipline

Load `references/kill-criteria.md` whenever a serious weakness appears. State triggered kills near the beginning.

Common hard kills include:

- no defensible mechanism or value-realisation logic;
- no credible evidence or data pathway;
- commodity framing with no non-consensus insight;
- weak or peripheral user role;
- identity dilution or severe portfolio fragmentation;
- high governance burden with no compounding asset;
- fragile collaborator, customer, student, or institutional dependence;
- effort-return mismatch;
- opportunity requires irreversible build before its central assumption can be tested;
- opportunity exists mainly because it is available;
- material ethical, reputational, authorship, data, confidentiality, or conflict risk.

For practice or venture opportunities, also apply the track-specific hard gates in `references/zero-to-one-management-practice.md`.

## Portfolio rules

Prioritise identity concentration and cumulative assets over activity count.

- **Platform-core:** can deepen the user's defining arena and produce cumulative assets.
- **Platform-adjacent:** adds useful scope, method, access, distribution, or option value without fragmenting identity.
- **Legacy/opportunistic:** legitimate but should not be mistaken for a strategic platform.

When comparing multiple opportunities, assess crowding, sequencing, complementarities, governance burden, and competition for the same high-value attention.

## Portfolio dispositions

Use one primary disposition. These are selection outcomes, not execution modes.

- **Core candidate:** deserves consideration for the user's defining portfolio and should proceed to Adaptive Commitment.
- **Selective candidate:** strategically attractive but conditional; requires Adaptive Commitment to determine whether and how to validate.
- **Service-only:** legitimate duty, public contribution, or relationship maintenance without core platform status.
- **Monitor:** potentially attractive, but current evidence is insufficient for portfolio entry.
- **Redesign:** the underlying problem may be valuable, but the present opportunity architecture is flawed.
- **Reject:** no justified place in the portfolio under current conditions.

Do not use Own, Build, Option, Validate, Scale, Delegate, Park, or Kill as Strategic Opportunity dispositions. Those are execution or allocation decisions for Adaptive Commitment.

## Output standards

A full general evaluation should include:

- verdict and confidence;
- strategic score and platform classification;
- portfolio disposition;
- deeper arena and non-consensus insight;
- strongest reasons to proceed;
- strongest reasons to decline or redesign;
- triggered kill criteria;
- user's recommended role, control, and ownership position;
- reusable assets and portfolio effects;
- principal uncertainty and binding weakness;
- evidence that would raise or lower the verdict;
- realistic counterfactual comparison;
- whether an Adaptive Commitment handoff is warranted.

For a management-practice or venture opportunity, add:

- Practice Impact score;
- Zero-to-One Venture score;
- original Seven Questions dashboard;
- hard-gate results and score caps;
- dual-track class: Build-to-Own Candidate, Impact Practice, Venture Hypothesis, or Redesign/Reject;
- book-wide stress-test findings that materially change the judgement.

Do not include detailed resource floors, caps, protected time, first actions, milestones, persistence periods, review dates, or reallocation rules.

## Lightweight modes

For a quick pass, provide only:

- verdict;
- provisional strategic score;
- platform fit;
- portfolio disposition;
- main rationale;
- main risk;
- principal evidence gap;
- handoff yes or no.

For portfolio review, group opportunities into:

- core candidates;
- selective candidates;
- service-only;
- monitor or redesign;
- reject.
