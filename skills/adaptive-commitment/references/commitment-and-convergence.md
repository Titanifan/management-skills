# Decision Convergence and Commitment Architecture

Use this module when the opportunity is attractive but the user continues comparing alternatives, delays starting, waits for urgency, or repeatedly reopens a decision.

## Core principle

Strategic choice does not require proving that one path dominates every alternative. Choose a sufficiently strong path under current information, commit within boundaries, improve it through execution, and retain pre-defined adaptation rights.

## Capability chain

Assess where this chain breaks:

`value recognition -> stop comparison -> bounded commitment -> first action -> low-urgency persistence -> evidence-based adjustment -> acceptance of foregone paths`

Do not assume strong analysis automatically produces commitment or execution.

## Decision-relevant information test

For every unresolved question, ask:

- Could the answer change the primary mode?
- Could it change the stage, resource level, owner, timing, or downside protection?
- Is the information obtainable at proportionate cost?
- Is the user learning, or seeking emotional certainty that no decision can provide?

Continue investigation only when the answer can materially change action.

## Stop-comparison rule

Stop active comparison when:

- hard kills have been checked;
- the principal uncertainty is explicit;
- no realistic alternative clearly dominates after full costs and risks are included;
- downside can be bounded;
- a milestone can generate the next required evidence;
- further information has lower expected decision value than beginning execution.

## Commitment architecture

Specify:

1. **Decision hypothesis:** what is believed and why.
2. **Primary mode and stage:** Own, Build, Option, Service-track, Delegate, Park, Kill, or Commercialise; Investigate, Validate, Build, Scale, or Reallocate.
3. **Resource floor:** minimum effort required for a fair test.
4. **Resource cap:** maximum exposure before review.
5. **Protected attention:** when and how core work will occur.
6. **Owner and role:** who is accountable and what the user will not own.
7. **First concrete action:** an observable action, not more abstract planning.
8. **Milestone and evidence standard:** what would change the decision.
9. **Persistence period:** how long execution continues without daily reconsideration.
10. **Review trigger:** date or condition.
11. **Transition rules:** evidence for scale, redesign, delegate, park, kill, or commercialise.
12. **Foregone alternatives:** which paths are suspended during the commitment period.

## Anti-crisis execution

The user may execute exceptionally well when choices disappear or consequences become urgent. Do not rely on this pattern. Create bounded urgency without manufacturing a crisis:

- protected calendar blocks;
- short milestone cycles;
- external accountability where useful;
- visible evidence requirements;
- a no-reconsideration interval;
- explicit consequences for missed milestones.

A milestone failure should trigger a pre-agreed response, not another vague extension.

## Counterfactual discipline

Evaluate alternatives using ex ante information and symmetrical assumptions. Include:

- upside and downside;
- probability and severity;
- transition cost;
- time to rebuild position;
- institutional and relationship effects;
- execution burden;
- what capabilities and favourable events would be required.

Do not compare the full cost of the chosen path with the ideal outcome of an unchosen path. Use counterfactuals to update decision rules, not to preserve infinite alternative identities.

## Self-image protection risk

Flag when under-commitment preserves the belief that the user could have achieved more if they had truly tried. This pattern protects optional self-images but prevents capability compounding and valid feedback.

Respond by designing a bounded fair test. Do not demand permanent total commitment.

## Output

Provide:

- whether comparison should continue or stop;
- the missing decision-relevant evidence, if any;
- commitment architecture;
- no-reconsideration period;
- first action;
- review and transition rules;
- foregone alternatives being suspended.
