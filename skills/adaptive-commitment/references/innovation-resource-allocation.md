# Innovation and Technology Resource Allocation

Use this module for AI adoption, emerging technologies, digital transformation, corporate innovation portfolios, technology commercialisation, innovation policy, mission-oriented policy, venture screening, PE value creation, or technology-to-value questions.

## Core position

Do not frame the task as accurately predicting the single winning technology. Improve the quality of staged resource allocation, capability building, validation, learning, and adaptive reallocation under uncertainty.

The generic question is:

> How should organisations allocate scarce capital, managerial attention, talent, and policy support across technological possibilities, and update those allocations as evidence develops?

## Dynamic pathway

Use this cycle:

`technological and institutional signals`
`-> staged commitment`
`-> capability building`
`-> operational or market validation`
`-> scale, redesign, park, or exit`
`-> value creation, capture, and distribution`
`-> learning and adaptive reallocation`

Do not assume value is an intrinsic property of the technology. Value may depend on timing, complementary assets, infrastructure, regulation, user readiness, organisational ownership, market formation, and distribution of costs and benefits.

## Evaluation dimensions

1. **Problem validity:** Is there an expensive, repeated, recognised problem or unmet need?
2. **Signal quality:** Are technical, customer, policy, cost, and competitive signals credible or mainly narrative and hype?
3. **Stage and timing:** Is the opportunity suitable for research, monitoring, pilot, capability building, or scale now?
4. **Technical feasibility:** Can the technology perform reliably in the relevant environment?
5. **Capability readiness:** Are data, talent, processes, governance, infrastructure, and absorptive capacity sufficient?
6. **Complementary assets:** Are distribution, integration, standards, suppliers, customer access, and institutional legitimacy available?
7. **Ownership and governance:** Is there a responsible owner, cross-functional authority, budget, and escalation path?
8. **Validation design:** What evidence will distinguish demonstration success from operational or market value?
9. **Scale pathway:** What must change for repeatable deployment across units, customers, or markets?
10. **Value realisation:** How will value appear in revenue, cost, risk, resilience, productivity, strategic position, or public outcomes?
11. **Value capture and distribution:** Who captures gains, who bears costs, and are material human-rights, environmental, labour, or inequality effects externalised?
12. **Residual learning:** If terminated, what knowledge, capability, data, relationships, or options remain?
13. **Adaptive reallocation:** What evidence triggers more investment, redesign, waiting, or exit?

## Portfolio governance

Do not evaluate projects in isolation when they compete for the same resources. Classify a technology portfolio into:

- **Monitor:** weak or early signal; maintain low-cost awareness.
- **Explore:** investigate a material uncertainty.
- **Option:** bounded pilot with resource cap and milestone.
- **Build:** create organisational capability and integration assets.
- **Scale:** expand after operational and value evidence is credible.
- **Redesign:** retain the problem or asset but change technology, ownership, use case, or business model.
- **Park:** wait for cost, infrastructure, regulation, or market readiness.
- **Kill:** stop when evidence is negative or opportunity cost is excessive.

Healthy innovation systems permit failure. The relevant question is whether failure was informative, proportionate, and acted upon.

## Technology-to-value diagnostic

For an individual project, test the chain:

`credible signal -> real problem -> capability readiness -> operational embedding -> adoption -> scalable economics -> value capture -> defensibility and responsible distribution`

Identify the first broken link. Do not recommend scale until the broken link is repaired or tested.

## Stakeholder-specific adaptation

- **Large enterprises:** emphasise portfolio governance, pilot-to-production transition, capability building, ownership, integration, and repeatable value.
- **Entrepreneurs:** emphasise problem validity, customer evidence, resource runway, complementary assets, and learning speed.
- **VC:** emphasise team, market, technical and institutional risks, but do not claim superior winner prediction without domain evidence, deal access, or proprietary data.
- **PE:** emphasise implementation within the holding period, operating capability, cash-flow or risk effects, and portfolio-company governance.
- **Policy makers:** include additionality, market formation, capability development, crowding-out, distribution, lock-in, public value, and exit or review conditions.

## Current market-entry hypothesis for the user

Treat the following as provisional, not permanent:

- A plausible first practice arena is large-enterprise AI and emerging-technology portfolio governance, particularly the gap between pilots and scalable value.
- The rationale is stronger distribution access through university, executive education, industry, policy, and alumni channels, plus fit with strategy, organisation, analytics, policy, and capability research.
- Validate this through customer interviews and pilots before building a company or broad product.
- Entrepreneurs may be useful pilot users; PE may become a later application; VC should not be the initial positioning without a narrow domain edge, proprietary evidence, or credible access.

## Output

Provide:

- portfolio or project stage;
- first broken link in the technology-to-value chain;
- commitment level justified now;
- capability and governance prerequisites;
- milestone and evidence standard;
- scale, redesign, park, or kill triggers;
- value capture and distribution implications;
- residual assets if the project fails.
