# Evidence Base for Adaptive Commitment

## Purpose and limits

Use this file to trace the management and organisational foundations of staged commitment, learning, attention allocation, escalation control, and adaptive reallocation. The Adaptive Commitment architecture is a synthesis. No cited source directly validates its seven layers, decision-mode taxonomy, resource-floor and resource-cap format, or review protocol as a complete system.

Several components are authorial heuristics. The Layer 0 biological and energy check is a practical capacity constraint, not medical guidance and not presented here as a construct derived from the selected management literature. Applications to academic careers, student supervision, and personal portfolios are cross-level translations that require calibration.

## Construct-to-rule map

| Construct or mechanism | Evidence provenance | Workflow translation | Boundary condition or failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exploration versus exploitation | March (1991) | Separate Investigate and Validate from Build and Scale. Maintain learning options without allowing exploration to consume the portfolio. | The literature does not specify a universal resource split or stage duration. |
| Incremental choice and option value | Bowman and Hurry (1993); McGrath (1997) | Break uncertain, partly irreversible commitments into stages with explicit evidence and exercise conditions. | A staged project is not necessarily a real option. Additional stages can add delay, governance burden, and false reassurance. |
| Entrepreneurial failure as information | McGrath (1999) | Evaluate a bounded experiment by information and residual assets as well as realised outcome. | Failure is valuable only when the experiment is informative and the downside is proportionate; this logic must not excuse weak design. |
| Escalation of commitment | Staw (1976); Brockner (1992) | Predefine resource caps, review conditions, and exit responses before identity, responsibility, and sunk costs intensify. | Persistence can be rational when new evidence improves prospects. A missed milestone is diagnostic, not an automatic kill. |
| Small wins and problem scale | Weick (1984) | Convert an overwhelming objective into a bounded first action that can produce visible evidence and reduce abstraction. | Small wins can fragment attention or optimise locally while leaving the governing problem untouched. |
| Attention structures | Ocasio (1997) | Treat protected attention, context switching, and governance channels as part of the commitment architecture. | Attention is socially and organisationally structured; calendars alone cannot remove political or relational demands. |
| Learning myopia | Levinthal and March (1993) | Review distant consequences, failures, and foregone alternatives rather than learning only from salient recent outcomes. | Review processes can themselves become ritualistic and may still reproduce biased samples of experience. |
| Fast strategic decision processes | Eisenhardt (1989) | Decision convergence need not mean less evidence: focus on decision-relevant information, alternatives, and real-time operating data. | Findings from high-velocity firms should not be generalised to every academic or policy decision; some choices legitimately require slow governance. |
| Dynamic managerial capabilities | Adner and Helfat (2003) | Reallocate only after assessing whether the actor has the cognition, human capital, and social capital required for the next stage. | Capability may be built, borrowed, or delegated; current weakness is not always a kill criterion. |

## Derived decision rules

The evidence supports six broad translations:

1. Increase commitment in stages when action can produce decision-relevant information.
2. State what evidence would justify exercising, redesigning, or abandoning an option.
3. Set review conditions before sunk cost, identity, and responsibility make reassessment harder.
4. Separate decision quality, execution fidelity, learning value, and realised outcome.
5. Treat attention and governance as scarce resources, not invisible overhead.
6. Review what the learning system overlooks: distant time horizons, distant alternatives, and failures.

The literature does not justify the exact seven-layer hierarchy, the primary decision-mode labels, or any numerical commitment threshold. Those elements are workflow design choices.

## Calibration cautions

- Do not label an indefinite pilot as Option without an expiry, evidence target, and resource cap.
- Do not use escalation research to rationalise premature abandonment.
- Do not treat every failure as learning; ask what belief changed and what asset remains.
- Do not infer that speed is always superior to deliberation.
- Do not convert health or energy observations into diagnosis.
- Recalibrate rules from repeated cases, not one vivid success or failure.
