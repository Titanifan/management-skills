# Commercial Opportunity and Academic Entrepreneurship

Use this module for business models, academic entrepreneurship, consulting diagnostics, executive education, AI tools, venture-screening processes, research platforms, or university-industry translation.

## Core discipline

Do not confuse an intellectually interesting framework with a commercial offer. Identify a specific customer, repeated and expensive problem, buyer, budget, distribution route, evidence standard, and implementation model.

Do not position the user as a general predictor of winning technologies. Prefer a defensible method for improving decisions, capability building, validation, governance, and adaptive resource allocation.

## Academic entrepreneurship modes

### Theory-to-method venture

Convert scholarly insight into a repeatable framework, diagnostic, workshop, training program, executive education module, or advisory method.

### Research-to-product venture

Convert research into software, data infrastructure, an AI product, dashboard, analytics tool, decision engine, or platform.

### Research-platform venture

Combine research, industry or policy engagement, student pipeline, datasets, thought leadership, and commercial services.

### Decision and portfolio diagnostic

Help a defined customer improve opportunity selection, staged commitment, project governance, evidence review, or resource reallocation.

## Commercial evaluation dimensions

1. **Customer pain:** Is the problem urgent, expensive, repeated, and recognised?
2. **Buyer and budget:** Who pays, from which budget, and why now?
3. **Distribution:** Can the user reach buyers through university, executive education, industry, government, alumni, professional networks, or digital channels?
4. **Method defensibility:** Is the offer difficult to copy because of theory, data, domain expertise, reputation, workflow integration, analytics, or accumulated cases?
5. **Decision effect:** Can the method change a real allocation, governance, capability, scale, or exit decision?
6. **Evidence pathway:** Can a pilot show better judgement, faster learning, lower waste, reduced risk, or improved outcomes?
7. **Implementation burden:** Can the customer use the method without unlimited bespoke consulting?
8. **Academic legitimacy:** Does it reinforce or damage the user's scholarly identity?
9. **Conflict and ethics:** Consider university duties, IP, student labour, confidentiality, editorial roles, and conflicts of interest.
10. **Residual assets:** Will pilots create cases, data, templates, methods, relationships, or distribution even if the offer changes?

## Decision modes

- **Commercialise:** repeated demand, evidence of use, buyer, distribution, and defensibility exist.
- **Build:** create cases, templates, datasets, workshops, or delivery capability.
- **Option:** run a bounded pilot with one customer group.
- **Redesign:** retain the customer problem but change the method, buyer, scope, or delivery model.
- **Park:** wait for distribution, evidence, capability, timing, or institutional clearance.
- **Kill:** no pain, buyer, willingness to pay, distribution, or defensibility.

## Commercial hard kills

- No clear customer or buyer.
- No recognised pain or decision consequence.
- No willingness to pay or budget pathway.
- No distribution channel.
- Generic productivity or consulting offer with weak moat.
- Claim depends on predicting winners without proprietary evidence or domain advantage.
- Full-time venture commitment is required before validation.
- Commercialisation undermines scholarly identity or creates material conflict.
- Implementation requires unbounded custom labour.

## Preferred pathway

Use staged commercialisation:

1. Problem interviews.
2. Case log.
3. Personal or team diagnostic.
4. Workshop prototype.
5. Executive education or advisory pilot.
6. Consulting diagnostic with measurable decision effects.
7. AI-assisted tool, dashboard, or workflow integration.
8. Research platform or venture after repeated demand and credible distribution.

## Current provisional application hypothesis

For the user's technology-to-value interest, a plausible first customer segment is large-enterprise strategy, innovation, digital, or AI leaders dealing with pilot-to-scale and portfolio-governance problems. Treat this as a hypothesis to test through interviews and pilots, not as a permanent system rule.

Entrepreneurs may offer accessible pilot cases but often have lower budgets. PE may offer stronger later value-creation applications after implementation evidence exists. VC should be approached only with a narrow domain edge, credible access, proprietary evidence, or a clear complement to existing investment judgement.

## Output

Provide:

- customer, user, buyer, and pain;
- decision affected;
- current commercial stage;
- minimum pilot and resource cap;
- evidence of value required;
- distribution and defensibility plan;
- conflict and governance constraints;
- scale, redesign, park, or kill conditions.
