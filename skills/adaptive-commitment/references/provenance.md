# Provenance Map for Adaptive Commitment

## Purpose and claim boundary

Use this file to distinguish scholarly constructs from cross-level workflow translations and repository design choices. The cited literature informs staged commitment, attention, learning, escalation control, and reallocation; it does not validate the seven-layer architecture, decision modes, resource contract, or review protocol as one system.

Several applications move from organisational or managerial research to academic careers, supervision, projects, and personal portfolios. Treat those moves as translations requiring case calibration. Layer 0 is a practical capacity check, not medical guidance or a diagnosis.

## Provenance labels

- `Scholarly-direct`: the source directly develops the named construct, mechanism, or distinction.
- `Scholarly-translated`: the executable rule adapts scholarship across level or context.
- `Practitioner-derived`: the rule comes from a practitioner framework whose assumptions must be tested.
- `Authorial-synthesis`: the repository combines sources into a workflow, score, threshold, classification, or artefact.
- `Case-calibrated`: repeated documented cases support a behaviour under stated conditions. No v0.2 element has this status.

## Source-to-rule map

| Element | Provenance | What the source supports | Executable use in this Skill | Boundary condition or failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exploration and exploitation | `Scholarly-direct` | Organisational learning involves competing exploration and exploitation demands (March, 1991). | Distinguish learning-oriented investigation from repeated build and scale. | The literature does not prescribe an individual resource split, stage sequence, or universal duration. |
| Incremental choice and option value | `Scholarly-direct` | Option reasoning can connect incremental choices to strategic investment under uncertainty (Bowman & Hurry, 1993; McGrath, 1997). | Ask whether a bounded next commitment can create information before an irreversible commitment. | A staged project is not necessarily a real option; stages can add delay, governance burden, or false reassurance. |
| Entrepreneurial failure as information | `Scholarly-direct` | Entrepreneurial failures can generate information relevant to future opportunities (McGrath, 1999). | Assess a bounded experiment by belief change and residual assets as well as outcome. | Failure is useful only when design is informative and downside is proportionate; the label must not excuse weak execution. |
| Escalation of commitment | `Scholarly-direct` | Responsibility, sunk costs, self-justification, and social or organisational pressures can sustain failing courses of action (Staw, 1976; Brockner, 1992). | Surface escalation risk before identity and responsibility intensify. | Persistence can remain rational when new evidence improves prospects; a missed milestone is diagnostic, not an automatic kill. |
| Small wins | `Scholarly-direct` | Recasting large problems into controllable small wins can reduce abstraction and support action (Weick, 1984). | Define a bounded first action capable of producing visible evidence. | Small wins can fragment attention, create local optimisation, or avoid the governing problem. |
| Attention structures | `Scholarly-direct` | Organisational rules, resources, and relationships shape attention (Ocasio, 1997). | Treat protected attention, context switching, and governance channels as resources. | Calendar protection alone cannot remove political, emotional, or relational demands. |
| Learning myopia | `Scholarly-direct` | Learning systems can privilege near, successful, and salient experience while neglecting distant alternatives and failures (Levinthal & March, 1993). | Review foregone alternatives, delayed effects, failures, and the sample of experience itself. | A review can become ritualistic and reproduce the same selective evidence. |
| Fast strategic decision processes | `Scholarly-direct` | High-velocity firms can make fast strategic decisions using focused information, alternatives, and real-time operating data (Eisenhardt, 1989). | Separate decision-relevant information from reassurance and endless comparison. | Findings from high-velocity firms do not imply that every academic, policy, ethical, or governance decision should be fast. |
| Dynamic managerial capabilities | `Scholarly-direct` | Managerial cognition, human capital, and social capital can shape strategic outcomes (Adner & Helfat, 2003). | Before deeper commitment, test whether required capability can be owned, built, borrowed, or delegated. | Current weakness is not automatically fatal, and corporate-level evidence does not validate an individual capability score. |
| Staged commitment rule | `Scholarly-translated` | Option, escalation, and learning research jointly make sequencing and evidence conditions relevant. | Increase commitment only when the next stage can produce or exploit decision-relevant evidence. | Indefinite pilots can preserve activity rather than option value; each stage needs an expiry and consequence. |
| Precommitted resource cap and review condition | `Scholarly-translated` | Escalation research motivates designing reassessment before sunk cost and self-justification intensify. | State the maximum commitment and the evidence for expansion, redesign, or exit before execution. | A cap can be arbitrary or prematurely constraining; material new evidence can justify revision. |
| Separate decision, execution, learning, and outcome | `Scholarly-translated` | Failure, learning, and escalation mechanisms show why realised outcome alone is an inadequate audit. | Review original reasoning, execution fidelity, belief change, residual assets, and outcome separately. | The separation must not make agents unaccountable for avoidable execution failure or harm. |
| Layer 0 capacity check | `Authorial-synthesis` | No listed management source validates the biological and energy screen. | Treat health, recovery, family rhythm, and energy as practical constraints on scope and timing. | Do not diagnose, medicalise, or infer that limited capacity makes an opportunity intrinsically weak. |
| Seven-layer hierarchy | `Authorial-synthesis` | The repository combines capacity, attention, identity, decision, convergence, execution, and learning into one architecture. | Prevent higher-level strategic recommendations from ignoring lower-level execution constraints. | The hierarchy has not been validated as a causal sequence and may not fit every decision. |
| Primary decision modes and stage overlay | `Authorial-synthesis` | Own, Build, Option, Service-track, Delegate, Park, Kill, Commercialise and the five stages are repository categories. | Force an explicit mode and distinguish investigation, validation, building, scaling, and reallocation. | Categories can overlap; labels must not replace analysis of the actual commitment. |
| Resource floor/cap commitment contract | `Authorial-synthesis` | No listed source specifies this format. | Make the minimum viable effort, maximum exposure, protected attention, owner, milestone, and evidence rule explicit. | False precision or an unrealistic floor can make the contract performative rather than governing. |
| Decision-convergence chain and review protocol | `Authorial-synthesis` | The repository combines decision-process, escalation, and learning ideas into a stopping and reopening rule. | Stop comparison when additional information is unlikely to change the choice; reopen on defined evidence or conditions. | Premature convergence can suppress legitimate dissent, ethics review, or slow-moving evidence. |

## Calibration rules

- Do not label an indefinite pilot as an Option without expiry, evidence target, and resource cap.
- Do not use escalation research to rationalise premature abandonment.
- Do not treat every failure as learning; state what belief changed and which asset remains.
- Do not infer that speed is always superior to deliberation.
- Do not convert capacity observations into diagnosis.
- Reserve `Case-calibrated` for repeated comparable cases with documented counterexamples.
