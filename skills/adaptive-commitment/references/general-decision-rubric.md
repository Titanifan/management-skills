# General Decision Rubric

Use this rubric for papers, grants, collaborations, platforms, industry engagements, commercial ideas, and major professional choices.

## Core dimensions

1. **Arena quality:** Does the opportunity open or deepen a valuable arena rather than add an isolated task?
2. **Signal strength:** Are there credible signals of non-consensus insight, unmet demand, policy timing, unique evidence, capability, or stakeholder pain?
3. **Stage fit:** Is the opportunity ready for investigation, validation, building, scale, or reallocation?
4. **Moat potential:** Could it build cumulative advantage through theory, constructs, data, method, capability, reputation, relationships, student pipeline, partner access, or distribution?
5. **Identity fit:** Does it strengthen the user's recognisable scholarly, supervisory, editorial, platform-building, or entrepreneurial identity without making identity rigid?
6. **Learning and option value:** Can a bounded commitment generate decision-relevant information at proportionate cost?
7. **Capability requirements:** What skills, data, infrastructure, processes, complementary assets, and governance must exist?
8. **Governance burden:** Will it create rescue work, emotional labour, unclear ownership, collaborator risk, student dependency, or administrative drag?
9. **Cognitive capital cost:** Will it consume deep attention, create context switching, or displace recovery and core work?
10. **Portfolio interaction:** Does it complement, sequence with, or crowd out other commitments?
11. **Feasibility:** Are evidence, time, roles, collaborators, student capability, customer access, and target expectations realistic?
12. **Residual asset value:** If it fails, will it leave reusable knowledge, theory, data, method, capability, relationships, tools, distribution, or learning?
13. **Downside and reversibility:** Can exposure be capped, and can the user exit or reallocate without unacceptable damage?

## Decision-quality tests

Do not infer decision quality from outcome alone. Ask:

- Was the evidence threshold reasonable at the time?
- Was the commitment proportionate to uncertainty?
- Were downside, governance, and opportunity cost considered?
- Was execution faithful to the chosen strategy?
- Did the review occur when promised?
- Was negative evidence acted upon?

## Classification guide

- **Own:** high arena quality, strong signals, strong moat and identity fit, with manageable burden.
- **Build:** strong capability or platform asset potential.
- **Option:** promising but uncertain; use a bounded test.
- **Service-track:** legitimate duty with limited strategic return.
- **Delegate:** worthwhile only if another person leads and the user's role is bounded.
- **Park:** insufficient readiness now; define a reactivation trigger.
- **Kill:** weak value, hard kill, excessive burden, or negative evidence.
- **Commercialise:** repeatable method or asset, strong pain, buyer, distribution, and evidence pathway.

## Confidence levels

- **High:** action is robust across realistic assumptions.
- **Medium:** enough evidence for bounded action, with material assumptions outstanding.
- **Low:** use investigation, option, or park rather than scale.

## Recommended response pattern

Give the verdict first. Then state mode, stage, role, principal uncertainty, resource commitment, milestone, evidence standard, review condition, and reallocation triggers.
