# Provenance Map for External Engagement

## Purpose and claim boundary

Use this file to distinguish research-supported constructs from executable translations and repository design choices. The cited literature informs components of External Engagement; it does not validate the complete access-to-relationship sequence, five-question dashboard, ownership taxonomy, free-work boundary, or node classifications as one predictive model.

Local rules for contracting, conflicts, procurement, IP, confidentiality, lobbying, research ethics, and promotion must be checked separately. The phrase “borrow the introduction; build the direct relationship” is repository language, not a quotation from a source.

## Provenance labels

- `Scholarly-direct`: the source directly develops the named construct, mechanism, or distinction.
- `Scholarly-translated`: the executable rule adapts scholarship across level or context.
- `Practitioner-derived`: the rule comes from a practitioner framework whose assumptions must be tested.
- `Authorial-synthesis`: the repository combines sources into a workflow, score, threshold, classification, or artefact.
- `Case-calibrated`: repeated documented cases support a behaviour under stated conditions. No v0.2 element has this status.

## Source-to-rule map

| Element | Provenance | What the source supports | Executable use in this Skill | Boundary condition or failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Individual academic and principal-investigator agency | `Scholarly-direct` | Researcher characteristics influence the variety and frequency of industry interaction; PIs can act as project managers, negotiators, resource acquirers, and boundary spanners (D'Este & Patel, 2007; Boehm & Hogan, 2013, 2014). | Treat the user's visible role and direct counterpart relationship as consequential. | Individual agency does not erase institutional ownership, team contribution, governance, or the introducer's credit. |
| Personal and institutional governance modes | `Scholarly-direct` | Personal contractual interaction and university-mediated interaction are distinct and can be complementary (Bodas Freitas et al., 2013). | Use institutional access without assuming the institution or intermediary must permanently mediate every exchange. | Direct contact can still require university contracting, approval, disclosure, or IP processes. |
| Trust, experience, and collaboration barriers | `Scholarly-direct` | Prior collaboration and inter-organisational trust can reduce orientation- and transaction-related barriers; relationship and institutional conditions influence collaboration success (Bruneel et al., 2010; Rybnicek & Königsgruber, 2019). | Design bounded early interactions that reveal reliability, alignment, and role clarity. | Trust is not permission for unlimited unpaid work and does not replace explicit governance. |
| Innovation intermediaries | `Scholarly-direct` | Intermediaries can search, connect, broker, translate, and support innovation processes (Howells, 2006). | Evaluate brokers by named introductions, problem intelligence, decision access, or funding pathways. | Convening activity and logo proximity do not prove conversion or relationship ownership. |
| Breadth of academic engagement | `Scholarly-direct` | Academic engagement includes collaborative research, contract research, consulting, and informal interaction and is broader than commercialisation (Perkmann et al., 2013, 2021). | Recognise several legitimate funding, research, policy, data, and relationship outcomes. | Engagement activity is not automatically valuable, productive, or strategically core. |
| Teaching-focused collaboration | `Scholarly-direct` | Teaching-focused university–industry collaboration can build student competencies and employability outcomes (Borah et al., 2021). | Keep teaching/WIL value distinct from evidence of funded research or a research-capable external relationship. | A teaching relationship can later convert, but duration or repeated semesters alone are not conversion evidence. |
| Industry and technological-field variation | `Scholarly-direct` | Public-research influence and university–industry interaction vary across industries, firms, and technological fields (Cohen et al., 2002; Meyer-Krahmer & Schmoch, 1998). | Treat sector wealth as a prior and inspect sponsor, absorptive capacity, data, authority, and resource contribution. | The cited evidence does not define a universal resource-readiness score for business-school engagement. |
| Policy engagement as a relational process | `Scholarly-direct` | Policy engagement advice emphasises relevant research, policy-process knowledge, accessibility, relationships, ground rules, and choices between advocacy and honest brokerage (Oliver & Cairney, 2019). | Identify policy stage, role, authority, independence, and follow-up pathway rather than treating a submission as the endpoint. | Engagement is labour-intensive, uncertain, politically situated, and not automatically impactful. |
| Career recognition of engagement | `Scholarly-direct` | Engaged work can be difficult to define, measure, report, and reward in promotion systems; dissemination and academic performance relationships are context-dependent (Smith et al., 2014; Jensen et al., 2008). | Maintain an attributable evidence trail covering initiation, leadership, resources, outputs, use, and follow-on activity. | Repository records do not determine institution-specific promotion credit. |
| Borrowed-access to direct-relationship rule | `Scholarly-translated` | Individual agency, complementary governance modes, trust, and intermediation jointly make direct substantive interaction relevant. | Borrow a legitimate introduction, then create direct communication and attributable contribution. | The rule must not become exclusion, appropriation of a colleague's relationship, or circumvention of institutional obligations. |
| Problem-owner and sponsor distinction | `Scholarly-translated` | Collaboration and intermediary research makes role, resource, and governance differences consequential. | Identify who understands or owns the problem separately from who can mobilise money, data, staff, authority, or policy action. | Roles can be distributed, change over time, or sit in a coalition rather than one person. |
| Resource-readiness and conversion test | `Scholarly-translated` | Trust, collaboration-success, engagement, and sector-variation findings support looking beyond access or organisation prestige. | Require observable reciprocal contribution before treating a relationship as project-ready. | Public-policy and community value may be real without payment; resource contribution can take several forms. |
| Relationship-conversion sequence | `Authorial-synthesis` | No listed study validates the complete ten-stage sequence. | Structure movement from access through trust, diagnosis, authority, co-definition, ownership, reciprocity, mechanism, and repetition. | Relationships can move non-linearly, regress, or legitimately stop at service, teaching, or public contribution. |
| Four-part ownership taxonomy | `Authorial-synthesis` | The repository separates relationship, problem, project, and output ownership. | Make directness, role centrality, and attribution explicit before substantial delivery. | Ownership is not necessarily exclusive and must respect team, counterpart, community, and institutional rights. |
| Five-question dashboard and node classes | `Authorial-synthesis` | No cited source specifies these questions, thresholds, or classes. | Distinguish resource-ready, gateway, both, conversion-test, teaching/service-only, and monitor/archive nodes. | The dashboard is an elicitation aid, not an empirically estimated score or forecast. |
| Free-work boundary | `Authorial-synthesis` | Trust research supports bounded relationship development but does not supply this delivery threshold. | Permit limited scoping and require an explicit mechanism before substantive analysis, reporting, or repeated delivery. | Some public-interest duties or deliberately chosen service work are legitimate; classify them honestly rather than treating them as conversion. |

## Calibration rules

- Separate documented evidence from inference for every named relationship.
- Do not infer conversion from longevity, meetings, logo visibility, or enthusiasm.
- Do not treat payment as the only valid resource signal or outcome.
- Do not equate directness with exclusive ownership.
- Do not generalise science- and engineering-heavy evidence without adapting mechanisms to business, policy, social-science, or community settings.
- Reserve `Case-calibrated` for repeated comparable engagements with documented stage movement and failure cases.
