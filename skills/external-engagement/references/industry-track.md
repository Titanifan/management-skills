# Industry and Commercial Track

## Purpose

Use this reference for direct firms, corporate decision-makers, commercial associations, investors, technology providers, consultancies, industry-funded research, and broker-assisted company access.

The objective is to move from a credible conversation to a direct, reciprocal, and attributable relationship with a persistent business problem and a resource pathway.

## Distinguish the actors

Do not collapse these roles:

- **User:** person who will use the output or method.
- **Problem owner:** person accountable for the business problem or decision.
- **Technical expert:** person who understands systems, data, operations, or implementation.
- **Champion:** person willing to advocate internally.
- **Sponsor:** person able to commit organisational resources or authority.
- **Economic buyer:** person or unit that controls the relevant budget or procurement decision.
- **Gatekeeper:** legal, procurement, data governance, cyber, HR, union, communications, or executive-office function.
- **Broker:** intermediary who can search, match, translate, convene, or introduce.

One person may occupy several roles. Verify rather than assume.

## Resource-readiness test

Evaluate resource readiness, not just sector wealth or company size.

Positive signals include:

- the problem appears in strategy, risk, investment, regulatory, operating, or transformation priorities;
- a named executive or business-unit leader owns the outcome;
- a budget category or procurement route exists;
- the organisation will contribute cash, data, staff, site access, users, or executive time;
- the organisation has internal R&D, innovation, strategy, sustainability, digital, policy, or transformation capability;
- the counterpart has previously commissioned research, consulting, trials, or evaluation;
- the organisation can join a credible grant or co-investment mechanism;
- implementation responsibility is defined;
- success can be observed through a real decision or workflow.

Negative signals include:

- interest is confined to a junior analyst with no escalation route;
- the request is a generic dashboard, model, or report with no owner;
- no one can say which decision will change;
- the organisation repeatedly seeks student or academic labour without staff or resource commitment;
- the only pathway is a grant with an imminent deadline;
- the counterpart asks the academic to build the internal business case before showing commitment;
- the organisation is wealthy but the issue is peripheral, unowned, or politically blocked.

## Industry qualification questions

### Problem

- What recurring decision or operational process is not working well?
- What is the economic, regulatory, safety, workforce, legitimacy, sustainability, or strategic consequence?
- How often does the problem recur?
- Which unit bears the cost?
- What evidence is currently missing?
- What has already been tried, and why did it fail?

### Authority

- Who is accountable for the outcome?
- Who can approve staff time, data, cash, procurement, or a pilot?
- Who may block implementation?
- What would the internal sponsor need to justify action?

### Fit

- Is the problem aligned with the user's expertise and research identity?
- Can the user contribute mechanism, diagnosis, method, evaluation, or governance rather than generic labour?
- Can the relationship produce research, policy, data, method, funding, reputation, or referrals?

### Conversion

- What is the smallest reciprocal next step?
- What will the organisation contribute before the user delivers substantive work?
- What mechanism fits: consultancy, contract research, sponsored research, grant, funded HDR, data partnership, workshop, or executive education?
- What would make the counterpart contact the user again directly?

## Access-mode guidance

### Direct company approach

Use when:

- a named problem owner is identifiable;
- the user's public expertise is credible and relevant;
- ownership value is high;
- no trusted intermediary is necessary.

First-contact objective:

- secure a short substantive conversation;
- test the problem hypothesis;
- avoid selling a pre-built project.

### Warm institutional introduction

Use when a colleague, centre, advisory board, alumni contact, or prior partner has a live relationship.

Require:

- a named introduction rather than permission to cold-contact;
- a reason the counterpart should meet the user specifically;
- direct user-counterpart follow-up after the introduction;
- accurate acknowledgement of the introducer;
- early clarification of the user's distinctive role.

Red flag:

- every communication must continue through the introducer, or the user is added only after the project is defined.

### Broker or intermediary route

Use brokers to reduce search, matching, translation, legitimacy, and coordination costs.

Ask the broker for:

- recurring member or client problems;
- named organisations facing those problems;
- the relevant senior problem owner;
- current funding or co-investment mechanisms;
- a direct introduction;
- information about previous university collaborations and why they succeeded or failed.

Do not ask only whether the broker has “companies interested in research”.

Broker conversion rule:

`broker credibility -> named introduction -> direct substantive relationship -> independently maintained relationship`

If the broker cannot provide a path beyond events, newsletters, generic directories, or student projects, classify it as low-conversion.

### Reactivation

For dormant relationships, determine:

- whether the original contact remains;
- whether the problem remains strategically relevant;
- whether a senior sponsor existed or only a single personal contact;
- whether the prior work produced value;
- whether another staff member can make a handover introduction.

Do not restart through a generic inbox if a warm institutional handover is possible.

## Teaching-client conversion test

A student-project client becomes research-capable only when at least one of these occurs:

- senior management joins problem definition;
- the organisation commits cash or credible in-kind resources;
- the problem persists beyond the semester;
- proprietary data are made available under research governance rather than only teaching delivery;
- a research publication, contract, grant, evaluation, or funded continuation is agreed;
- the organisation introduces the user to another resource holder.

Otherwise classify it as teaching/service-only, regardless of the number of semesters.

## First-conversation structure

1. Establish why the conversation is relevant now.
2. Ask the counterpart to describe the decision or friction in operational terms.
3. Probe recurrence, stakes, failed attempts, and affected stakeholders.
4. Identify the problem owner and sponsor.
5. Offer a concise reframing, not a solution package.
6. Test whether the counterpart sees value in a second, more focused discussion.
7. Close with a reciprocal next step, such as data availability check, internal sponsor introduction, or one-page joint problem framing.

## Conversion-test options

Use one bounded test when evidence is incomplete:

- request introduction to the sponsor;
- ask the organisation to nominate staff and data owners;
- ask whether the issue has a budget or procurement route;
- propose a short co-definition meeting with the decision owner;
- ask whether the organisation would contribute cash or formal in-kind support to a grant;
- ask whether a student prototype would be followed by funded evaluation or implementation;
- ask whether publication, case use, or impact attribution is permitted.

A vague “let's stay in touch” is not a conversion signal.

## Funding-pathway selection

### Consultancy or advisory

Prefer when the problem is well-defined, decision time is short, and the organisation seeks tailored judgement or analysis.

### Contract or sponsored research

Prefer when new evidence, data collection, evaluation, or theory-informed research is required and the organisation can fund it directly.

### Collaborative grant

Prefer when:

- the relationship and problem are already credible;
- the organisation has a named sponsor;
- cash and in-kind contributions are realistic;
- the research question is independently valuable;
- the scheme fits the timeline and governance burden.

### Funded HDR or postdoctoral pathway

Prefer for persistent multi-year problems with data, supervision, and organisational commitment.

### Data or case partnership

Prefer when publication and access create sufficient mutual value, but formalise governance, confidentiality, use rights, attribution, and continuation expectations.

### Executive education or workshop

Prefer when the need is capability building and a repeatable method can be delivered without unbounded custom work.

## Industry hard questions

Before recommending substantive effort, answer:

- Who will lose money, time, legitimacy, safety, or strategic position if nothing changes?
- Who can commit resources?
- What contribution will the organisation make before delivery?
- Why is the user essential rather than interchangeable?
- What will the user own or be visibly credited for?
- What is the next decision that should change?
- What happens if no funding mechanism appears?
